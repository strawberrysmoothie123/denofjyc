## 1. ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY

US equity markets ended the final trading session of Q2 2026 under modest pressure as a hotter-than-expected PCE inflation print, a continued tech-and-chip selloff fueled by an OpenAI IPO delay report, and hawkish Fed commentary combined to pressure growth stocks for a fifth consecutive session on the Nasdaq. The S&P 500 slipped 0.05% to 7,354.02 while the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.24% to 25,297.62 — its fifth straight losing day and a weekly decline of 4.6% — as investors continued rotating out of AI and technology names into defensive sectors and small caps. The May PCE price index came in hotter than expected at +0.4% MoM and +4.1% YoY (highest since April 2023), with core PCE at +3.4% YoY slightly above the 3.3% consensus, cementing the case for the Warsh Fed's hawkish pivot; Minneapolis Fed President Neel Kashkari reinforced that signal by announcing he now expects one rate hike this year (reversing his prior forecast of one cut). ON Semiconductor cratered 22.58% on a surprise $7 billion Synaptics acquisition announcement, and chip stocks broadly remained under selling pressure on fears that AI infrastructure spending is plateauing. The Dow Jones Industrial Average held relatively flat at 51,876.11 (-0.09%), the Russell 2000 small-cap index outperformed, and WTI crude oil dropped sharply (~4%) as US-Iran peace negotiations advanced toward a framework deal — the fourth consecutive session of oil declines below $70/barrel. For Q2 2026 as a whole, the market week ended with the S&P 500 down nearly 2% on the week and the Nasdaq down 4.6%, though both indices remain well up year-to-date, with the AI and energy-driven bull market in place despite a week of significant distribution in mega-cap technology.

## 2. MAJOR NEWS, EVENTS & ANNOUNCEMENTS

### A. PCE Inflation (May 2026) — Hotter Than Expected
The Bureau of Economic Analysis released the May 2026 Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) report on Friday morning, delivering a hawkish upside surprise. The headline PCE price index rose **+0.4% month-over-month** and **+4.1% year-over-year** — the fastest annual pace since April 2023. Core PCE (excluding food and energy) came in at **+3.4% YoY**, slightly above the 3.3% consensus forecast. Encouragingly, personal income rose a robust **+0.7% MoM** (vs. +0.4% estimate) and personal spending also climbed **+0.7% MoM** (vs. +0.6% estimate), reflecting a consumer that remains resilient despite elevated prices. The personal saving rate held at **3.0%**. Real (inflation-adjusted) spending rose +0.3% and real incomes turned positive for the first time in four months (+0.3%), suggesting the consumer is not yet capitulating. However, the elevated headline number confirming prices are rising at their fastest pace in three years immediately pushed Fed rate-hike probability higher and weighed on rate-sensitive growth stocks at the open.

### B. Fed's Kashkari Flips to Rate Hike Forecast
Minneapolis Federal Reserve President Neel Kashkari made a significant hawkish pivot on Friday, stating that he has reversed his earlier outlook and now expects **one interest rate increase by year-end 2026** — a complete 180 from his March forecast of one rate cut. Kashkari cited energy-price-induced inflationary pressures stemming from continued conflict and supply disruptions in the Middle East as the primary driver. His comments, combined with the hot PCE print, reaffirmed the hawkish tone set by the FOMC under Chair Kevin Warsh at the June 17 meeting, where the dot plot was revised upward to signal at least one additional potential hike. The 2-year Treasury yield eased modestly (-4 bps to 4.13%) as markets digested the news — a somewhat counterintuitive move that reflected the competing disinflationary signal from falling oil prices and the drop in 5-year Michigan inflation expectations rather than outright dismissal of Kashkari's warning.

### C. OpenAI IPO Delay — AI Sentiment Hit
A report from The New York Times indicated that OpenAI is leaning toward **delaying its highly anticipated initial public offering to 2027**, citing SpaceX's underwhelming post-debut performance and elevated volatility in AI-related shares as reasons for caution. The report acted as an immediate dampener on AI sentiment across the market: chip stocks, which had already been under sustained selling pressure this week, extended losses, and the broader AI ecosystem (cloud providers, infrastructure names) felt incremental selling pressure. The OpenAI delay report is significant not just for the IPO itself but as a signal that even AI insiders are pausing to reassess valuations and market receptivity — a contrast to the FOMO-driven AI investing environment that prevailed earlier in 2026.

### D. ON Semiconductor's Synaptics Acquisition (-22.58%)
ON Semiconductor (ON) announced a **$7 billion acquisition of Synaptics**, a deal that stunned markets and sent ON shares plummeting **22.58%** in one of the largest single-session declines in semiconductor sector history. The market reaction reflected deep skepticism about strategic fit and timing: acquiring a human interface semiconductor company at a major premium when ON's own shares have been under pressure from cyclical concerns struck analysts as value-destructive. The deal immediately triggered downgrades or cautious notes from several banks. SanDisk, Lam Research, and Western Digital fell as much as 10% in sympathy, as investors rotated out of the broader memory-adjacent chip space.

### E. Apple's Continued Decline; Microsoft Rebounds Partially
Following Thursday's shocking announcement of iPhone and Mac hardware price increases, **Apple (AAPL) continued to slide**, with shares down approximately **6.12%** over the two-day stretch since the pricing news. The stock has become a focal point for the narrative that AI memory costs are creating a structural headwind for consumer hardware — Cupertino's rare unscheduled mid-cycle repricing signals that memory cost inflation is flowing through to end-product pricing faster than the market expected. **Microsoft (MSFT)**, by contrast, partially recovered on Friday, gaining **+2.34%**, as investors distinguished between Microsoft's diversified cloud-and-enterprise revenue streams (less exposed to consumer hardware margin risk) versus Apple's more hardware-centric model. Nvidia, Tesla, and Oracle each declined more than 1%, while Alphabet fell 0.30%-0.84%.

### F. US-Iran Peace Talks Progress; Oil Under Pressure
US-Iran diplomatic negotiations advanced for a fourth consecutive session, with reports pointing to a potential framework agreement. WTI crude oil futures for August delivery fell approximately **4.1%** and Brent crude fell approximately **4.5%**, extending a multi-session downtrend that has taken crude below $70 per barrel. The sustained drop in energy prices carries dual implications: (1) disinflationary pressure that partially offsets the hot PCE print, and (2) earnings headwinds for the energy sector and petrocurrencies. The geopolitical risk premium that had been embedded in crude since earlier this year is visibly deflating as diplomatic progress unfolds.

### G. Q2 2026 Final Trading Day — Quarter-End Context
June 26, 2026 marked the **final trading session of the second quarter of 2026**. Quarter-end and month-end rebalancing flows were active throughout the session, contributing to intraday volatility and helping to cushion the downside in equities despite the adverse macro data. Pension funds and portfolio managers rebalancing toward target allocations likely provided technical buying support, particularly in equities that had underperformed bonds during the quarter. Despite the rocky final week, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq remain significantly positive year-to-date, reflecting the AI- and energy-driven bull market that characterized much of 2026.

### Rate Market Narrative

Treasury yields presented a nuanced picture on Friday: despite the hot PCE print that would normally push yields sharply higher, the 10-year yield moved lower (estimated ~-5 bps toward 4.40%) and the 2-year yield also eased 4 bps to 4.13%. This counterintuitive reaction reflects several competing forces.

**Why yields fell despite hot PCE:** (1) The 5-year University of Michigan inflation expectation dropping to 3.3% — a 0.6 percentage point decline — suggests longer-term inflation psychology is not becoming unanchored, which matters more for the bond market than near-term data beats. (2) Oil's 4%+ decline acts as a forward-looking disinflationary signal, and bond markets rapidly priced in the prospect of moderating headline PCE in coming months if energy prices remain subdued. (3) Quarter-end demand for fixed income created technical buying pressure from institutional rebalancers. (4) The flight-to-quality bid from equity weakness provided incremental treasury demand.

**Yield Curve:** The curve maintained its positive slope, with the 10-2 spread at +27 bps and the 10-3M spread at +62 bps. This "normal" (non-inverted) yield curve is a significant departure from the 2023-2024 inversion and represents an improving growth outlook even as the Fed tilts hawkish.

**Fed Funds Rate Context:** The Fed held rates at 3.50%-3.75% at the June 17 FOMC meeting under Chair Kevin Warsh. The dot plot revision to signal at least one additional hike — now reinforced by Kashkari explicitly forecasting a hike — means markets are pricing approximately 40-50% probability of one hike before year-end. The hot May PCE at 4.1% YoY (highest since April 2023) solidifies the case for Fed patience at minimum, and raises the bar for any pivot toward cutting rates in 2026. Wells Fargo economists had pre-flagged a potential +0.5% MoM PCE print; the actual +0.4% MoM was somewhat below that hawkish fear scenario, which may have reduced the yield spike.

**Key driver for rates:** Lower-than-feared magnitude of PCE month-on-month (+0.4% vs. +0.5% feared) + oil price decline + falling 5yr inflation expectations + quarter-end buying = yields lower despite hot YoY PCE. Net hawkish signal kept yields elevated relative to earlier in June.

### Dollar Narrative

The US Dollar Index edged marginally higher (+0.06%) to 101.68, a modest uptick driven by the hot PCE data which reinforced the relative hawkishness of the Warsh Fed vs. global central banks. However, the dollar's gains were capped by the contradictory signal from falling Treasury yields (which reduce the US rate premium) and the risk-positive read from declining oil prices (lower energy costs reduce global inflation, moderating the safe-haven bid for the dollar). The DXY remains in a consolidation zone around 101-102, and the PCE data alone was insufficient to break the dollar materially higher given the competing disinflationary signals.

**Key driver**: Hot PCE supports dollar marginally; offset by lower Treasury yields and oil price decline reducing inflation risk premium.

### Gold — $4,023.40 (-0.60%)

Gold continued to retreat from its recent highs, falling 0.60% to $4,023.40. Despite crossing the psychologically significant $4,000/oz threshold in recent weeks amid earlier inflation concerns, gold is now in a corrective phase as: (1) oil price declines reduce near-term inflation fears; (2) the prospect of a Fed rate hike (rather than cut) increases the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding gold; (3) geopolitical risk premium deflates as US-Iran peace talks advance. Gold remains well above its long-term average but is facing headwinds from the rate hike repricing environment.

**Key driver**: Oil-driven disinflation narrative + rate hike expectations (higher opportunity cost) + geopolitical risk premium deflating = gold under pressure.

### WTI Crude Oil — ~$70.69/bbl (~-4.1%)

West Texas Intermediate crude fell sharply for the fourth consecutive session, trading near or below $70/barrel. The sustained decline reflects: (1) meaningful progress in US-Iran peace negotiations that could eventually bring additional Iranian supply to global markets; (2) demand concerns from the weakening consumer sentiment data (Michigan at 49.5, historically low) suggesting macro softening; (3) technical momentum — crude breaking below the $70 support level is attracting systematic/momentum sellers. The energy price decline is one of the most important macro developments of the week, as it creates a powerful disinflationary counterweight to the otherwise-hawkish PCE data, and reduces Middle East risk premium across assets.

**Key driver**: US-Iran peace talks framework progress → supply risk premium deflating; technical breakdown below $70 key support; demand concerns from low consumer sentiment.
