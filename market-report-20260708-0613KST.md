## 2. MAJOR NEWS, EVENTS & ANNOUNCEMENTS

### A. Trump Declares Iran Ceasefire "Over," US Resumes Strikes

The session's dominant catalyst was President Trump's comment at the NATO summit in Ankara, Turkey that he considers the interim US-Iran memorandum of understanding to be "over," following a series of Iranian attacks on commercial vessels — including a Qatari LNG tanker — transiting the Strait of Hormuz. The US responded with fresh military strikes on Iranian targets and the Treasury Department revoked the waiver that had allowed Iran to sell crude on international markets. Trump added that further strikes could continue "tonight," keeping headline risk elevated into the close. The escalation ended a period of relative market calm around the conflict and reintroduced a geopolitical risk premium across equities, oil, and rates simultaneously — a combination strategists flagged as unusual, since Treasuries typically rally on this kind of headline risk but instead sold off as inflation concerns dominated.

### B. Oil Surges as Strait of Hormuz Risk Re-Escalates

Crude prices posted their sharpest one-day gain in weeks on the back of the Iran news: WTI crude closed up 4.4% at $73.52/bbl (a two-day gain of more than 7%), while Brent crude jumped roughly 5.3% to near $78.11/bbl intraday. The Strait of Hormuz handles roughly a fifth of global oil flows, and traders priced in a higher probability of continued disruption given the US revocation of Iran's sanctions waiver and the prospect of further American strikes. Energy was the only S&P 500 sector to close higher on the day (+2.42%), providing a rare pocket of strength as most other sectors declined in sympathy with the broader risk-off tone.

### C. FOMC Minutes Show Fed Split 9-9 on 2026 Rate Hike, Warsh Stays Silent

The Fed released minutes from its June 16-17 meeting at 2:00 PM ET, revealing that policymakers were evenly divided nine-to-nine on whether an additional rate hike would be warranted before year-end 2026, with Chair Kevin Warsh — presiding over his first meeting since taking over from the prior chair — again declining to offer his own rate projection, leaving the meeting transcript as the only on-the-record institutional view on the committee's reaction function. The Fed held its target range at 3.50%-3.75%. Analysts characterized the minutes as confirming a "higher-for-longer" bias reinforced by the same-day oil spike, with Fed funds futures markets nudging up the odds of a hike later this year rather than pricing in cuts. The minutes' hawkish framing, combined with the oil-driven inflation scare, was a key driver of the day's broad-based rise in Treasury yields.

### D. Dollar and Gold React Unusually to Risk-Off Move; Bonds Offer No Safe-Haven Bid

Rather than the classic flight-to-safety pattern, Wednesday's session saw Treasury yields rise (not fall) alongside falling equities, as investors treated the oil-driven inflation risk as more consequential for portfolios than the geopolitical shock itself. The dollar index firmed to its highest level in about a week near 101.17, drawing some safe-haven support, while gold — which would typically be expected to rally on a Middle East escalation — instead fell more than 1% to around $4,050/oz, its lowest level since July 2, as rising real-yield and higher-for-longer rate expectations outweighed safe-haven demand for bullion.

### After-Hours Activity (Post-Close July 8)

After-hours trading was dominated by earnings and AI-infrastructure-adjacent names rather than the Iran headlines: Penguin Solutions jumped roughly 26.6% after a strong quarterly earnings report, while Broadcom (+5.87%), Arista Networks (+7%), Akamai Technologies (+7.9%) and Alibaba's US-listed shares (+11.6%) were among the largest post-close gainers. During the regular session, notable individual movers included Walmart (+0.8%) after announcing price cuts on staples such as ground beef and Coca-Cola products; Cognizant Technology Solutions (+6.2%) on news of an expanded partnership with Google Cloud for enterprise Gemini AI adoption; and Rivian Automotive, which plunged 18.1% after announcing a 75-million-share Class A stock offering that diluted existing holders.
