## 1. ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY

US equity markets staged a broad, tech-led relief rally on Monday, June 29, as two macro catalysts converged to lift investor sentiment after a punishing prior week: a closely-watched Supreme Court ruling in a 5-4 vote blocking President Trump from removing Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook—explicitly carving out an exception for Fed independence while expanding executive power over other agencies—and Alphabet's much-anticipated debut as a Dow Jones Industrial Average component, replacing Verizon. The Dow crossed 52,000 for the first time in its history, closing at 52,182.74 (+0.59%), while the Nasdaq Composite surged 2.07% to 25,820.15 as Magnificent Seven names including Tesla (+8.45%), Alphabet (+4.96%), Amazon (+3.18%), and Meta (+2.2%) rebounded sharply from five consecutive losing sessions. A fragile US-Iran ceasefire held ahead of Doha peace talks, keeping oil prices subdued (WTI ~$69.70), which supported credit conditions and pushed Treasury yields meaningfully lower across the curve. With Q2 ending Tuesday and a holiday-shortened jobs week ahead—June nonfarm payrolls move to Thursday ahead of July 4—the market's forward focus shifts squarely to the labor market.

## 2. MAJOR NEWS, EVENTS & ANNOUNCEMENTS

### A. Supreme Court Blocks Trump from Firing Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook

In the session's dominant catalyst, the US Supreme Court ruled 5-4 to block President Trump's attempt to remove Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook while her lawsuit challenging the firing proceeds through the courts. Chief Justice John Roberts, joined by Justice Brett Kavanaugh and the three liberal justices (Kagan, Sotomayor, Jackson), authored the majority. The ruling was a landmark decision: while the court simultaneously expanded the president's authority to remove officials at other independent agencies, it explicitly carved out an exception protecting Federal Reserve board members from dismissal.

Markets reacted sharply and positively. The ruling removes the single largest tail risk overhanging US financial markets since early 2026—namely, the possibility that the Trump administration could effectively commandeer Fed policy by placing loyalists on the board. Equity futures had already been elevated in pre-market trading, and the official open accelerated gains. Treasury yields, which had briefly traded near multi-week highs early in the session, rallied strongly (prices up, yields down) as bond markets priced out the risk premium for potential political interference with monetary policy. The dollar softened modestly in tandem.

Analysts at Evercore ISI noted: "This ruling is the best possible outcome for risk assets—it gives the Fed the political cover it needs to make data-driven decisions without the shadow of removal hanging over the board." JPMorgan strategists echoed: "The carve-out for the Federal Reserve preserves the credibility of US monetary policy and removes a key discount factor that had been keeping multiples compressed."

### B. Alphabet Debuts in the Dow Jones Industrial Average, Replacing Verizon

Effective at the open of Monday's session, S&P Global added Alphabet (GOOGL) to the 30-stock Dow Jones Industrial Average, replacing Verizon (VZ). Alphabet's Class A shares (GOOGL) join Nvidia, Amazon, Apple, and Microsoft as mega-cap technology representatives in the blue-chip index. S&P Global said the inclusion would bolster the Dow's exposure to artificial intelligence, cloud infrastructure, and digital advertising themes.

GOOGL shares surged 4.96% to $350.24, providing one of the largest single-point boosts to the price-weighted index given its elevated share price. Verizon fell 5.24% on its removal—a reflexive rebalancing effect as index funds sold VZ and bought GOOGL en masse at the open. Alphabet's index debut comes at a notable moment: the stock is staggering toward its worst month since February 2025, and the broader Mag7 cohort endured a 4.6% Nasdaq weekly loss through last Friday, partly driven by reports that OpenAI is considering delaying its IPO due to SpaceX's post-debut underperformance and AI share volatility.

The composition shift marks a structural change in the Dow, which has historically underweighted technology. Strategists at Goldman Sachs noted the Dow's tech weighting now approaches the S&P 500's, making the index more reflective of the modern US economy.

### C. US-Iran Ceasefire Holds Ahead of Doha Peace Talks

After weeks of escalating military exchanges following US strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities in spring 2026, both Washington and Tehran agreed to halt mutual attacks ahead of peace negotiations scheduled this week in Doha, Qatar. The fragile ceasefire, originally brokered in mid-June with the framework calling for an immediate cessation of hostilities, suspension of sanctions on Iranian oil sales, and release of $24 billion in frozen Iranian assets, continues to strain under intermittent provocations (CNN reported June 28 that Bahrain and Kuwait had been targeted by Tehran). However, the Doha talks signal a potential de-escalation, and energy markets are pricing in an improvement.

WTI crude oil traded around $69.70/barrel—rebounding modestly from a four-month low but well off pre-conflict highs. Brent settled near $72. Lower energy prices have materially improved the US credit picture: investment-grade and high-yield spreads tightened, and Treasury yields fell as the oil-driven inflation premium unwound. The Strait of Hormuz, which carries approximately one-fifth of global oil supply, has functioned normally during the ceasefire period.

### D. Rocket Lab to Acquire Iridium in $8 Billion Deal

Space communications company Rocket Lab (RKLB) announced it will acquire Iridium Communications (IRDM) in a deal valued at approximately $8 billion. RKLB surged 15.9% on the day, while IRDM soared 25.4% on the takeover premium. The deal would give Rocket Lab access to Iridium's established constellation of 66 low-earth-orbit satellites and its commercial satellite-phone subscriber base, dramatically expanding Rocket Lab's addressable market beyond launch services and into operational satellite networks. The acquisition is subject to regulatory review but was well received by analysts as a strategic acceleration of Rocket Lab's ambitions in the satellite communications sector.

### E. Prior Week's Tech Selloff Context

The session's strong relief rally followed one of the worst weeks for large-cap technology in 2026. The Nasdaq Composite posted five consecutive losing sessions through Friday June 26—its worst weekly loss at -4.6%—while the S&P 500 shed approximately 2% for the same period. Catalysts for last week's selloff included: (1) a New York Times report that OpenAI is considering delaying its IPO to 2027 due to SpaceX's lackluster post-IPO trading and overall volatility in AI-related equities; (2) Apple and Microsoft announcing consumer price increases on iPhone and Xbox products, raising concerns about consumer demand elasticity under the tariff environment; and (3) continued chip-stock rotation out of hyperscalers. The Dow, by contrast, rose 0.6% last week as investors rotated into defensive and value-oriented names.

### F. After-Hours Activity (Post-Close June 29)

No major after-hours earnings releases of note were scheduled for the June 29 session. The primary post-market focus remained on positioning ahead of Q2/half-year-end rebalancing on Tuesday June 30, which is expected to generate elevated trading volumes as pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, and institutional investors reset portfolio weights.

### S&P 500 (7,436.08, +1.12%)

The S&P 500 ended a five-session slide with a broadly constructive session, recovering more than a third of the prior week's ~2% decline in a single day. The index was lifted primarily by the Communication Services (+4.1%) and Consumer Discretionary (+3.5%) sectors, with Tech (+2.6%) close behind. The Supreme Court's Fed independence ruling was the critical morning catalyst: futures that had been pricing in a modest open spiked materially higher after the ruling became public, as equity risk premiums compressed in response to reduced institutional uncertainty. Sector breadth was positive, with 8 of 11 S&P sectors advancing. Defensives—Utilities, Consumer Staples, Healthcare—lagged or declined modestly as capital rotated back into growth.

Technically, the S&P 500 recaptured its 20-day moving average at ~7,400 intraday and closed above it for the first time since June 23. Strategists at Morgan Stanley flagged this as a near-term positive: "The 7,400 level acted as resistance-turned-support, and a clean close above it reopens the path to the 7,500–7,600 range. However, Q2-end flows and Thursday's jobs print remain the near-term gatekeepers." With the index still roughly 2-3% below all-time highs, the path back to record territory depends heavily on whether labor data validates the soft-landing narrative.

### Nasdaq Composite (25,820.15, +2.07%)

The Nasdaq's 2.07% gain was the sharpest single-day advance since mid-May, reversing more than 40% of the prior week's steep 4.6% loss. The outsized move was driven almost entirely by Mag7 and large-cap tech names rebounding from oversold conditions: Alphabet +4.96%, Tesla +8.45%, Amazon +3.18%, Meta +2.2%, Microsoft +1.8%, and Apple +1.4%. Nvidia, which had been particularly battered by the OpenAI IPO delay narrative (reducing near-term AI GPU demand forecasts), also recovered ~1.7%.

The Nasdaq-100 (NDX) outperformed the broader Composite, rising approximately 2.25%, reflecting the concentrated mega-cap weighting. Analysts noted that Friday's quarter-end rebalancing by large institutional investors—which typically forces selling of outperforming assets (tech) to buy laggards—may have artificially depressed the Nasdaq through last week, and Monday's reversal partly reflects that mechanical pressure abating. Wedbush Securities' Dan Ives called the bounce "expected and warranted: the AI investment cycle is intact, the valuations on names like Tesla and Alphabet had become disconnected from fundamentals."

Tesla's 8.45% move was the session's most eye-catching large-cap rally. No specific Tesla-specific catalyst emerged, but the stock had shed over 15% in the prior two weeks and short interest had climbed significantly; the combination of short-covering and rotation into beaten-down high-beta names amplified the move.

### Dow Jones Industrial Average (52,182.74, +0.59% — NEW ALL-TIME RECORD HIGH)

The Dow Jones Industrial Average crossed 52,000 for the first time in its 130-year history, settling at 52,182.74 for a record close. The milestone was powered principally by Alphabet's index debut—as a higher-priced stock, GOOGL's 4.96% gain contributed more index points than any other component, given the Dow's price-weighted methodology. Verizon's 5.24% drop, as index-tracking funds mechanically rotated, was an offsetting drag but was more than compensated by the broader tech strength.

Notable contributors included Boeing (+1.9%), Salesforce (+2.1%), and IBM (+1.4%), while traditional defensives like Johnson & Johnson and Procter & Gamble were roughly flat. The Dow's relatively muted percentage gain versus the Nasdaq reflects its lower tech weighting even after Alphabet's addition, as well as the continued underperformance of energy and telecom components (ExxonMobil -0.5%, Verizon -5.24%).

The record close represents a meaningful psychological milestone and arrives at the start of what could be a historically significant stretch: the upcoming July 4 holiday week is traditionally a low-volume, positive-bias period for US equities.

### Russell 2000 (~2,961.51)

The Russell 2000 underperformed large-cap indices on the day, reflecting the session's dominant narrative—tech and mega-cap leadership—rather than broad risk appetite. Small caps have shown relative resilience in 2026 through much of the year, with the index's total market capitalization growing from $2.7 trillion at the 2025 Russell reconstitution to $3.5 trillion in 2026. However, Monday's rally was driven by index-specific catalysts (Alphabet/Dow, Supreme Court ruling) that benefited large caps disproportionately. Notably, Friday June 27 marked the annual Russell Reconstitution—the last before LSEG switches to a twice-yearly reconstitution schedule—which itself generated elevated late-session volatility and volume as forced rebalancing trades concentrated into the final minutes. SpaceX also joined the Russell 1000 as part of that reconstitution.

### VIX (17.65)

The Cboe VIX fell from prior readings near 18-20, reflecting the meaningful reduction in near-term uncertainty following the Supreme Court ruling on Fed independence. While a VIX at 17.65 remains slightly elevated relative to the 14-15 lows seen in spring 2026, it has retreated from the "VIX flirting with 20" conditions that marked last week's tech selloff. Options markets suggest participants continue to hedge around the jobs data Thursday and Q2-end flows, keeping the VIX somewhat elevated versus a fully calm market environment.

### Bond Market Narrative

Monday's bond market delivered a broadly bullish session—prices rose, yields fell—in an unusual but important alignment with equities. The dual drop in both yields and stocks-correlating implied volatility reflects the asymmetric nature of Monday's catalysts: the Supreme Court ruling removed a structural uncertainty from financial markets rather than simply improving economic growth expectations.

The 2-year Treasury yield fell the most (-9.1 bps to 4.088%), consistent with markets adjusting monetary policy expectations. With the Fed Funds rate held at 4.25-4.50% since early 2026, the 2-year trading below the effective Fed Funds rate of ~4.33% implies the market is pricing meaningful probability of rate cuts over the next 12 months. Earlier in the session, the 2-year briefly touched a 52-week high near 4.22% before the Supreme Court ruling broke the tension and sent yields sharply lower—a dramatic intraday reversal that illustrated how much rate-path uncertainty had been embedded in near-term Treasuries.

The 10-year yield at 4.375% sits near the mid-range of its 2026 trading band (roughly 4.10-4.70%), having pulled back sharply from the elevated levels seen during the peak of US-Iran conflict escalation in April-May. The decline in 10-year yields is also consistent with the easing of oil-driven inflation expectations: WTI crude near $69.70 (down from ~$90+ during peak hostilities) has meaningfully reduced breakeven inflation rates.

The yield curve shape remains modestly positively sloped (10yr - 2yr spread ≈ +28.7 bps), having un-inverted earlier in 2026. This normalization is viewed constructively by credit markets as it reduces pressure on bank net interest margins. However, the 30-year at 4.865% continues to embed a meaningful term premium as investors demand compensation for long-duration duration risk in an environment of elevated federal deficit spending.

**FOMC Outlook:** No Fed officials spoke publicly on June 29. Markets currently price approximately 1.5 rate cuts by year-end 2026, with the first cut most likely in September depending on the trajectory of labor data. Thursday's June jobs report—forecast at +118K nonfarm payrolls, down from May's 172K—will be critical. A reading at or below 100K would likely accelerate rate-cut pricing and push yields meaningfully lower; a reading above 150K would push back cut expectations and lift yields.

### Dollar & FX Narrative

The US dollar softened modestly on Monday (DXY -0.03% to 101.33), with the greenback's slight decline driven by the same Supreme Court ruling that lifted equities. The Federal Reserve independence ruling removed a key tail risk for the dollar: markets had been pricing a small but non-trivial probability that political interference at the Fed could eventually force the central bank into easier monetary policy, which would have been dollar-negative over a medium-term horizon. Paradoxically, the ruling that protects the Fed's credibility also removes some of the risk premium that had supported the dollar as a "safe haven" against US institutional uncertainty.

The weekly DXY performance tells a slightly different story: the dollar gained approximately 0.73% for the week ending June 29, buoyed by relative US economic resilience (May jobs: 172K) and the geopolitical risk premium from US-Iran tensions that historically supports the dollar as a reserve currency. The daily -0.03% decline on June 29 represents a modest giveback of that geopolitical premium as the ceasefire appeared to hold.

**Cross-asset implications:**
- **Gold:** Likely softened modestly on the dollar-positive Supreme Court outcome (reduced monetary policy uncertainty), partially offset by lower real yields supporting gold prices.
- **Oil (WTI ~$69.70):** Crude remained near four-month lows as the US-Iran ceasefire supported a return of Iranian crude to global markets (suspended sanctions on Iranian oil sales). Brent settled near $72. Lower energy prices are broadly supportive of risk assets and compress the inflation risk premium in rates markets.
- **Emerging Markets:** A softer dollar on the day is broadly constructive for EM currencies and assets, reducing the debt-service burden for dollar-denominated EM sovereign debt. Asian equities that had sold off during the US-Iran conflict escalation (Japan's Nikkei fell as much as 284 points at one point Monday, -0.41%) faced some headwinds despite the US rally, partly reflecting the complex geopolitical dynamics in the region.
- **USD/JPY:** The yen was broadly steady to slightly firmer against the dollar as the dual rally in US stocks and bonds (unusual) reduced the rate-differential case for yen selling. Bank of Japan intervention risk continues to limit yen depreciation, with BOJ officials having signaled discomfort with yen weakness.
