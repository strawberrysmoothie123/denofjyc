### 1.1 Main Drivers of Market Index Moves

**Primary Catalyst — Korean Chip Market Crash:**
South Korea's KOSPI plunged 9.99% (down 910.71 points to 8,203.84) overnight, triggering automatic circuit breakers twice during the session — an event so severe it echoed across global markets by the NY open. Samsung Electronics fell 12.3% and SK Hynix fell 12.5%, with foreign investors liquidating a combined 5.79 trillion won (~$3.8 billion) in a single session. Together these two companies account for ~48% of the KOSPI's market cap. The South Korean Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) publicly warned about systemic risks posed by leveraged single-stock ETFs linked to the two chipmakers — a statement that amplified panic. The root cause: fears that AI hyperscaler capital expenditure would not generate commensurate returns on investment, leading investors to question the semiconductor "supercycle" narrative.

**Secondary Catalyst — Bank of America Hawkish Rate Hike Note:**
In a note published June 22 and widely circulated Tuesday morning, BofA economist Aditya Bhave reversed the bank's prior forecast of steady rates, now calling for 75 basis points of Fed rate hikes across three meetings — September, October, and December — lifting the federal funds rate to 4.25%–4.50%. BofA cited: sticky inflation described as "unambiguously worse," labor market resilience, and the unexpectedly hawkish posture of new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh. Warsh, in last week's FOMC press conference, repeatedly stressed price stability and indicated current policy is "not especially restrictive." Deutsche Bank issued a similar revision calling for a September rate hike. CME FedWatch now prices in: 72.8% probability of a September hike, 80.6% for October, and 87.9% for December.

**Market Rotation:** Risk-off rotation pushed investors into defensive sectors (Healthcare +1.41%, Consumer Staples +1.87%) and into bonds (providing a mild yield cap). Financials (+0.34%) were slightly positive as rising rate expectations benefit net interest margins.

### 4.2 Key Rate Drivers

**Flight to Safety Offset Rate Hike Fears:** Despite the BofA/Deutsche Bank hawkish revisions, bond yields were largely unchanged on the day. Two forces canceled each other:
- **Upward pressure:** Revised expectations for Fed rate hikes pushed short-end yields higher.
- **Downward pressure (flight to safety):** Equities selloff — particularly the sharp drop in risk assets and semiconductor stocks — prompted investors to rotate into Treasuries as a safe haven, capping 10-year yield at ~4.5%.

**Net Result:** Yield curve moved almost nothing. The 10Y-2Y spread remained slightly positive (~27 bps), continuing the slow normalization from the inverted curve of recent years.

**FOMC Backdrop:** New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's FOMC press conference last week struck a more hawkish tone than markets expected, repeatedly emphasizing price stability and the risk that monetary policy may not be "especially restrictive." This has reset rate expectations sharply higher.

## 10. Key Takeaways & Forward Outlook

1. **The AI trade is under pressure.** The Korean chip crash and US semiconductor selloff (-6.5% in SMH) signal that markets are beginning to question whether AI infrastructure spending will deliver sufficient returns. This is a regime change risk for the 2025-2026 AI bull run.

2. **Rate hike risk is now the consensus.** BofA's three-hike call, validated by Deutsche Bank and CME FedWatch data above 70-80% probability, means the "rates higher for longer" narrative has firmly re-established. This is particularly negative for high-duration growth stocks (tech, semis).

3. **Defensive rotation has begun.** Healthcare (+1.41%) and Consumer Staples (+1.87%) outperforming while Technology sold off confirms classic late-cycle positioning.

4. **Micron earnings (June 24 AH) are a critical test.** Given the 11.4% pre-earnings selloff, expectations are recalibrated. A strong print and margin guidance above 80% could stabilize the semiconductor space; a miss or cautious guidance could deepen the sell-off.

5. **PCE data (June 25) is the macro pivot.** If May PCE confirms sticky/elevated inflation, the BofA three-hike scenario gets further validation, putting additional pressure on equities. A soft print offers some relief.
