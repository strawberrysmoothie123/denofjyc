## 1. ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY

US equities closed higher on Tuesday, June 30, capping the best quarterly performance for the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite since 2020, as a semiconductor-led rally extended Monday's relief surge into the final session of the first half. The S&P 500 gained 0.79% to 7,499.36, the Nasdaq Composite jumped 1.52% to 26,213.72, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 0.26% to a fresh record close of 52,319.20, with chip names like Intel and AMD surging roughly 7% as the iShares Semiconductor ETF climbed 3.6% amid heavy AI-infrastructure-driven demand and quarter-end institutional rebalancing/window-dressing flows. The VIX eased further to roughly 17 as volatility continued to unwind from last week's tech-driven selloff, while the dollar index firmed to around 101.3 even as gold slid to roughly $4,020/oz (its worst quarter since Q2 2013) and oil extended a steep quarterly decline (WTI near $70, down about 24% for Q2) on easing Strait of Hormuz tensions. After the close, Nike delivered a mixed reaction—shares fell more than 4% in extended trading despite revenue and adjusted-EPS beats, as investors looked past a one-time $986 million IEEPA tariff-refund benefit to underlying China weakness—while attention now turns to Wednesday's ISM Manufacturing PMI, a speech from new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, and Thursday's early-released June jobs report ahead of the holiday-shortened week.

## 2. MAJOR NEWS, EVENTS & ANNOUNCEMENTS

### A. Wall Street Closes Out Best Quarter Since 2020 on a Chip-Fueled Rally

Tuesday marked the final session of Q2 2026, and US equities delivered one of their strongest quarterly performances in six years. The S&P 500 finished the quarter up roughly 13–14%, its best quarterly return since the pandemic-recovery surge of 2020, while the Nasdaq Composite advanced over 22% for the quarter (the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index posted its best quarter on record, up an estimated ~88%). The Dow Jones Industrial Average notched its best quarterly gain since 2022 and its best first-half performance since 2021, helped by Monday's addition of Alphabet to the index. For the first half of 2026 overall, the Dow gained roughly 8.9%, the S&P 500 about 9.6%, and the Nasdaq more than 12%, with the Russell 2000 posting its best first half since 1991.

The session itself was driven by a renewed rush into chip stocks: the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) climbed 3.6%, with Intel and AMD each jumping roughly 7%, extending year-to-date gains of approximately 277% and 163%, respectively, as AI infrastructure spending continued to power broad semiconductor demand. Apple also contributed meaningfully to index gains, adding an estimated $110 billion in market capitalization on a roughly 2.6% advance. Strategists attributed part of the move to mechanical quarter-end and half-year-end portfolio rebalancing and "window dressing," in which institutional managers buy recent winners (chips, mega-cap tech) ahead of reporting deadlines—flows analysts characterized as "real but partly cosmetic."

Despite the strong finish, commentary cautioned that the underlying picture was more mixed than the headline gains suggest: across June, the "Magnificent Seven" hyperscalers as a group fell roughly 10%, with Alphabet and Amazon down about 17% and 19% from their May peaks, Microsoft off more than a third from its October highs, and Meta down nearly 30% from its August peak — underscoring that June's rally was concentrated in semiconductors and select large-caps rather than broad-based mega-cap strength.

### B. HSBC Flags Dollar, Treasury and AI-Trade "Pain Trades" for Second Half of 2026

HSBC strategists published a note warning investors to brace for several "pain trades" — scenarios where prevailing consensus positioning could be wrong-footed — heading into the second half of the year. The bank's most prominent call: the dollar could stage an "explosive" rally that catches offside investors who have largely positioned for dollar weakness on the assumption the Fed will cut rates; HSBC noted such a move could accelerate quickly if the Fed adopts a more hawkish stance than markets currently price, or if geopolitical risks flare again. The bank also warned oil-price shocks tied to Middle East instability could keep inflation elevated and produce a steeper Treasury curve than consensus expects (rather than the bull-flattening many investors anticipate), and argued the AI trade's "pain trade" risk is actually further upside, since full-year 2026 AI-related earnings growth expectations have already been marked down to flat-to-lower than the prior year's pace — leaving room for positive surprises. This note matters because it directly informs the cross-asset read for the quarter-end session, where the dollar firmed even as equities and risk appetite improved — an unusual combination that HSBC's framework helps explain as positioning-driven rather than purely macro-driven.

### C. Wall Street Reshuffles Ratings Ahead of Earnings Season

Tuesday brought a wave of analyst repositioning ahead of the upcoming Q2 earnings season. Piper Sandler issued a notable double upgrade on Block (XYZ) to Overweight from Underweight, lifting its price target to $100 from $58, citing the fintech's profitability push beginning to pay off. Oppenheimer cut Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs to Underperform from Perform and downgraded Citigroup and Bank of America to Perform from Outperform, primarily on valuation grounds after the group's strong run. Deutsche Bank upgraded Comcast (CMCSA) to Buy from Hold (while trimming its price target to $32 from $34) following Comcast's announcement that it will spin out its media business, which Deutsche Bank said sets up the stock to re-rate higher. JPMorgan added cybersecurity name Tenable Holdings to its Analyst Focus List, calling it "the best positioned exposure management vendor." Jefferies reiterated Broadcom as a Buy, telling clients to "buy the weakness" given the pullback since its last earnings report, while HSBC downgraded Fortinet to Reduce. Goldman Sachs upgraded Tradeweb Markets to Buy from Neutral with a $146 target, and Daiwa upgraded Honeywell to Outperform from Neutral, raising its target to $255 from $240. Rosenblatt also published its list of favorite stocks for the second half of 2026, part of a broader wave of sell-side repositioning around the quarter turn.

### D. Oil and Gold Extend Steep Quarterly Declines as Hormuz Tensions Ease

Commodity markets continued to reflect the easing of the US-Iran standoff that has defined the quarter. WTI crude slipped toward $70/barrel, on track for a roughly 24% decline for the quarter (about 19% for the month of June alone), as the resumption of US-Iran peace talks in Doha and rising tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz (oil flows there have risen to an estimated 4.8 million barrels per day since the interim deal) eased the supply-disruption premium that had been embedded in prices. Brent crude traded near $74/barrel, down roughly 30% for the quarter — its largest quarterly decline since 2020. Gold held near $4,020/oz, close to its weakest level since November 2025, down about 11% for the month and roughly 14% for the quarter — gold's first quarterly loss since 2024 and its steepest quarterly drop since Q2 2013 — as a firmer dollar and reduced safe-haven demand (following the de-escalation in the Middle East) weighed on bullion.

### E. After-Hours Activity (Post-Close June 30)

**Nike (NKE) — Q4 FY2026 Earnings:** Nike reported fiscal Q4 revenue of $10.972 billion, ahead of the $10.859 billion consensus estimate, with GAAP EPS of $0.72 versus $0.13 expected. The outsized GAAP beat was driven almost entirely by a one-time $0.52/share benefit tied to an expected recovery of IEEPA tariffs after the Supreme Court struck down a substantial portion of the administration's global tariff regime, contributing to a roughly $986 million tariff-refund benefit and an 8.9-percentage-point boost to gross margin. On an adjusted basis (excluding the tariff item), EPS came in at $0.20 versus $0.13 expected — a genuine beat, but a much smaller one than the headline GAAP number implies. Greater China revenue fell 12% year-over-year to $1.30 billion, though this still topped Wall Street's $1.24 billion estimate. Despite the top- and bottom-line beats, NKE shares fell more than 4% in extended trading, whipsawing between gains and losses as investors weighed the underlying China weakness and the non-recurring nature of the tariff benefit against the headline numbers.

**Constellation Brands (STZ):** Shares climbed 3.7% after fiscal Q1 results beat consensus EPS estimates by $0.18 on revenue of $2.43 billion, with management issuing confident FY2027 EPS guidance of $11.20–$11.90.

**Bloom Energy (BE):** Shares spiked 11% after the company announced its strategic financing partnership with Brookfield was expanded from $5 billion to $25 billion, to fund large-scale fuel-cell power projects globally — explicitly targeting AI data center power demand.

**Transocean (RIG):** Shares rose 2% after the offshore driller secured a $1 billion-plus, seven-rig-year contract with Equinor to deploy three harsh-environment semisubmersible rigs on the Norwegian shelf.

### S&P 500 (7,499.36, +0.79%)

The S&P 500 extended Monday's relief rally to close the quarter just shy of the 7,500 milestone, finishing roughly 13–14% higher for Q2 2026 — its best quarterly performance since the pandemic-recovery rally of 2020. Tuesday's gain was driven primarily by continued strength in technology and communication services, as the same semiconductor-led rotation that lifted the Nasdaq spilled into the broader index; Apple alone contributed an estimated $110 billion in market cap on a roughly 2.6% gain. Index-level breadth was supported by quarter-end institutional rebalancing flows, which strategists noted were "real but partly cosmetic," as fund managers bought outperforming sectors ahead of mid-year reporting deadlines. The index's first-half gain of roughly 9.6% came despite a volatile macro backdrop that included an oil-price shock from the US-Iran conflict and intermittent AI-spending jitters, underscoring what analysts described as resilient underlying earnings growth — first-quarter S&P 500 earnings grew roughly 18% year-over-year, with the median constituent delivering its strongest earnings surprise in four years. Looking ahead, strategists flagged that forward P/E multiples have actually eased modestly even as the index rallied, implying earnings growth — not pure multiple expansion — has been the primary driver of 2026 gains, though valuations in technology specifically remain viewed as elevated.

### Nasdaq Composite (26,213.72, +1.52%)

The Nasdaq was the session's standout performer, rising 1.52% to close out a quarter in which the index gained more than 22% — its best quarterly performance in six years — powered by a historic run in semiconductor stocks (the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index's best quarter on record). Tuesday's session-specific catalyst was a broad chip-sector bid: the iShares Semiconductor ETF gained 3.6%, with Intel and AMD each surging approximately 7% on continued AI-infrastructure demand. This stood in contrast to the underlying weakness in mega-cap "Magnificent Seven" names over the month of June — as a group those stocks fell roughly 10% in June alone, with Microsoft, Meta, Alphabet, and Amazon all well off their respective 2026 peaks — highlighting that June's Nasdaq strength was concentrated in semiconductors rather than broad mega-cap leadership. The divergence reinforces a theme several desks have flagged: the AI trade has rotated from software/hyperscaler capex narratives toward the chipmakers directly supplying that infrastructure.

### Dow Jones Industrial Average (52,319.20, +0.26% — fresh record close)

The Dow notched another record close, building on Monday's milestone first close above 52,000, to finish its best quarter since 2022 and its best first half since 2021 (+8.9%). Tuesday's modest 0.26% gain was more muted than the broader market's advance, consistent with the index's comparatively lower technology weighting even after Monday's addition of Alphabet as a replacement for Verizon. The session's gains were broad enough to extend the record but not dramatic, reflecting a market in which mega-cap technology and industrials provided support while more cyclically defensive Dow components lagged the chip-driven rally playing out in the Nasdaq.

### Russell 2000 (~3,010, approximately flat)

Small caps were essentially flat on the session (Russell 2000 finishing up roughly 0.01%), even as the index notched its best first-half performance since 1991, having reached an intraday all-time high near 3,034 earlier in the quarter and finishing the first half up over 20% year-to-date — outpacing the S&P 500's roughly 7.5% first-half return at one comparison point during the quarter. Tuesday's flat finish reflects that the session's specific catalyst — the semiconductor rally — disproportionately benefits large-cap and mega-cap constituents rather than the broader small-cap universe, leaving the Russell to mark time at quarter-end after a historically strong first half driven by a "great rotation" narrative of capital moving from concentrated mega-cap tech into a broader swath of domestically-focused small caps.

### VIX (~17, down from 17.65 prior close)

The Cboe Volatility Index extended its decline from Monday's 17.65 close (itself down 4.0% on the day), continuing to unwind the elevated volatility that characterized last week's tech-led selloff. Analysts noted that quarter-end turns are historically associated with elevated money-market and liquidity volatility, but the Federal Reserve's ample liquidity provisioning this cycle has kept that risk muted, consistent with the VIX holding in the mid-to-high-16/low-17 range through the close of Q2 rather than spiking on calendar-driven flows. The continued grind lower in the VIX alongside gains across all three major indices reflects a market pricing in low near-term event risk ahead of Wednesday's ISM data and Thursday's early-released jobs report.

### Bond Market Narrative

Treasury yields were little changed on the quarter's final session, holding near the levels reached after Monday's sharp rally, which had been driven by the Supreme Court's ruling preserving Federal Reserve independence (the Lisa Cook removal case). With that tail risk already priced out a day earlier, Tuesday's session lacked a fresh rates-specific catalyst, and the curve largely tracked sideways as the equity rally proceeded on its own, sector-specific (semiconductor) momentum rather than a renewed repricing of the rate path. The 2-year yield continues to trade modestly below the effective Fed Funds rate, consistent with markets pricing in a notable probability of rate cuts over the coming 12 months — current consensus sits around 1.5 cuts priced by year-end 2026, with the first move most likely in September, contingent on labor-market data.

Looking ahead, HSBC's second-half "pain trade" note (covered in Section 2) is notable context for the rates outlook: the bank argued that consensus is too confident in a flattening curve, and that renewed Middle East-linked oil price volatility could keep both headline and core inflation elevated enough to produce a steeper curve than markets currently expect. That view stands in some tension with the quarter's dominant narrative of falling oil prices (WTI down roughly 24% for Q2) easing inflation breakevens — a dynamic that has been the primary driver of the meaningful pullback in yields from the elevated levels seen during the peak of the US-Iran conflict in April–May. The market's next major test for the rate path comes with Wednesday's ISM Manufacturing PMI and Thursday's early-released June jobs report, both of which will shape expectations heading into the July 28–29 FOMC meeting.

### Dollar & FX Narrative

The dollar index firmed modestly to around 101.3, extending its second consecutive monthly advance and capping a month in which the DXY gained more than 2% — its strongest monthly performance since July of last year. The dollar's quarter-end strength is a notable counterpoint to the broader narrative of Fed rate-cut expectations that has dominated 2026, and it comes even as equities rallied — an unusual combination that HSBC's research note (Section 2) frames as a potential "pain trade" setup: with most investors positioned for dollar weakness on the assumption of Fed easing, any hawkish surprise from new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh or a renewed bout of Middle East-linked inflation risk could trigger an outsized, "explosive" dollar rally that catches the market offside. For now, the firmer dollar appears to be reflecting a combination of relative US growth resilience, quarter-end positioning, and a partial unwind of the geopolitical risk premium that had built up during the spring's US-Iran escalation.

**Cross-asset implications:**
- **Gold (~$4,020/oz):** Bullion remained pinned near its weakest level since November 2025, posting its worst quarterly performance since Q2 2013 (down roughly 14% for Q2) as the firmer dollar and easing safe-haven demand — following the de-escalation in the Strait of Hormuz standoff — removed two of the key supports that had driven gold's earlier-2026 strength.
- **Oil (WTI ~$70, Brent ~$74):** Crude extended one of its steepest quarterly declines on record (WTI down ~24%, Brent down ~30% for Q2) as rising tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz (now estimated at 4.8 million barrels per day) signaled the effective normalization of flows following the US-Iran interim agreement. Lower energy prices have been a key tailwind for risk assets all quarter, compressing inflation breakevens and supporting the bond rally that has unfolded since the conflict's peak in April–May.
- **Emerging Markets:** A firmer dollar is typically a headwind for EM currencies and dollar-denominated EM debt service costs; however, the broader risk-on tone (record US equity closes, falling volatility) has partially offset that pressure heading into the second half.
- **USD/JPY (~148):** The yen held roughly steady against the dollar, with persistent Bank of Japan intervention-risk rhetoric continuing to act as a floor against further yen weakness even as US-Japan rate differentials remain a structural headwind for the currency.
