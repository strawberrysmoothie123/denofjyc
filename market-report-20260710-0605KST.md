## 2. MAJOR NEWS, EVENTS & ANNOUNCEMENTS

### A. SK Hynix's Record $26.5 Billion Nasdaq Debut Headlines the Session
South Korea's SK Hynix, the world's second-largest memory chipmaker and a critical Nvidia HBM supplier, priced its American depositary receipt offering at $149 and opened for when-issued trading at $170 — a 14% pop — before closing its first Nasdaq session up 13% at $168.01 under ticker "SKHYV" (the ticker converts to "SKHY" for regular trading next week). The $26.5 billion raise, funding aggressive new fab and equipment investment, is the largest-ever US listing by a non-US company and drew what underwriters described as multiple-times oversubscribed demand. Chairman Chey Tae-won told CNBC "it's a kind of dream, and now it's a dream come true," citing "enormous" demand for HBM capacity tied to the AI buildout. The listing lifted sentiment across the broader memory and semiconductor-equipment complex, which has been one of 2026's standout groups: Micron is up more than 200% year-to-date, while Lam Research, Marvell Technology and Intel have each more than doubled.

### B. Nvidia and Meta Lead a Strong Big-Tech Week
Nvidia rose roughly 4% and was a key support for the S&P 500's advance, while Meta Platforms jumped about 6% Friday to cap a nearly 15% weekly gain — its best weekly performance since early 2024. The Meta move followed Bank of America reiterating its buy rating after an internal company memo, reviewed by Reuters, suggested Meta is improving its artificial-intelligence cost structure, easing investor concern about the sustainability of its AI capital-spending ramp. The combination of the SK Hynix listing and the Big Tech rally reinforced a market narrative that AI-related capital spending and monetization remain the dominant driver of large-cap equity performance heading into Q2 earnings season.

### C. Iran Conflict De-escalation Hopes Pull Oil and Volatility Lower
Crude prices fell Friday — WTI settled at $71.41/bbl (-0.93%) and Brent at $76.01/bbl (-0.38%) — even though both benchmarks still finished the week sharply higher (Brent +5.5%, WTI nearly +4%) on the earlier US-Iran escalation. The reversal came as traders grew hopeful that shipping through the Strait of Hormuz would gradually normalize following the end of the latest round of tit-for-tat air strikes, alongside reports that Washington and Tehran could resume talks next week. Analysts cautioned the underlying conflict remains unresolved and could still upend the International Energy Agency's forecast for a significant 2027 oil surplus if hostilities resume. The cooling geopolitical risk premium also fed directly into equities: the VIX fell a further 5.11% to 15.03, its second consecutive sharp decline, as options markets priced out much of the tail-risk hedging demand built up earlier in the week.

### D. Delta Air Lines Opens Airline Earnings Season With Record Fuel Costs
Delta Air Lines reported second-quarter revenue of $17.67 billion, up 14% year-over-year and ahead of expectations, with adjusted EPS of $1.56 also beating consensus. However, net profit fell roughly 25% year-over-year to $1.6 billion as average fuel price per gallon surged 75% year-over-year to $3.93 — the highest quarterly fuel expense in the company's history — squeezing margins even as pre-tax profit held at $1.4 billion. Delta affirmed full-year guidance of $6.50-$7.50 adjusted EPS and $3-4 billion of free cash flow, and announced a 15% dividend increase beginning in the September quarter, a signal of management confidence in demand despite the cost pressure. The print serves as an early, mixed read on summer travel demand and cost inflation heading into the broader Q2 earnings season, which accelerates next week with major bank results.

### E. Yen Strengthens on Pension-Fund Reallocation Comments
The Japanese yen strengthened toward the 161-per-dollar level Friday, nearly reversing its losses from earlier in the week, after Japanese Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama said the government would encourage domestic pension funds to increase their holdings of Japanese financial assets. USD/JPY fell roughly 0.4% to 161.70. The yen had been trading under persistent pressure near multi-decade lows for most of the week, and markets continue to watch for signs of formal Bank of Japan or Ministry of Finance intervention given the scale of the US-Japan rate differential.

### After-Hours / Pre-Market Activity
Delta's results were released before Friday's open rather than after Thursday's close, setting an early tone for the session. Post-close Friday trading was comparatively quiet for large-caps, with mega-cap tech names little changed; among smaller names, Tutor Perini (TPC) surged after a major contract win and WD-40 (WDFC) rose on a 24% jump in quarterly net sales, while a handful of small caps sold off sharply on earnings misses — a bifurcated tape consistent with a market rotating away from index-level catalysts into single-stock earnings reactions ahead of next week's bank-earnings kickoff.
