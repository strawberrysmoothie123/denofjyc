## 2. MAJOR NEWS, EVENTS & ANNOUNCEMENTS

### A. Samsung's Blowout Profit Still Disappoints Sky-High AI Bar (Overnight Catalyst)

The session's key overnight catalyst was Samsung Electronics' Q2 report (Korea time Tuesday morning): operating profit surged roughly 18-19x year-on-year, surpassing both Nvidia's and Apple's quarterly profits and beating consensus, with management guiding for an approximate 1,800% jump in operating profit on booming AI-memory (HBM) demand. Despite the eye-popping headline numbers, Samsung shares slid about 8% as investors judged the results insufficient to justify the AI-chip sector's recent valuation run-up — a "priced-for-perfection" reaction that set a cautious tone heading into the US open. Asia chip shares broadly slid on the read-through, and the selling carried directly into the US semiconductor complex.

### B. DeepSeek's Own AI Chip Push Rattles the Nvidia Supply-Chain Narrative

Reuters reported that China's DeepSeek has begun designing its own AI inference chip — a roughly year-long effort aimed at reducing reliance on Nvidia and Huawei — still in early stages but expanding its chip-design team with semiconductor partners. Combined with separate reports that Meta is weighing commercializing its excess computing capacity, the news prompted investors to reassess the durability of the AI infrastructure buildout even though near-term demand signals remain strong. The one-two punch of the Samsung reaction and the DeepSeek report drove a broad semiconductor selloff: Sandisk fell roughly 11%, Micron and Intel each dropped about 8-9%, Marvell Technology fell about 9%, and AMD slid about 7%, with KLA and Broadcom also posting declines; the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) fell more than 3% on the session.

### C. Nvidia Denies Kyber AI-Server Delay Report, Shares Rebound

Nvidia shares reversed higher intraday (+1%+, back above $198) after the company pushed back on a SemiAnalysis report claiming its next-generation "Kyber" AI server rack had suffered "massive delays," pushing the launch from 2027 to 2028. A company spokesperson told Yahoo Finance "our roadmap remains intact," directly refuting the claim. The denial helped cap losses in the broader chip complex, and Goldman Sachs separately reiterated a bullish stance on the stock, calling its valuation of roughly 21.7x forward earnings "compelling" — a rare bright spot in an otherwise negative day for semiconductors.

### D. Iran Oil Sanctions Reimposed After Strait of Hormuz Attacks — Crude Surges

The Trump administration reimposed sanctions on Iranian oil sales after a series of attacks on commercial vessels near the Strait of Hormuz, with the Treasury Department revoking an earlier temporary sanctions-relief waiver and giving buyers of Iranian crude until July 17 to unwind transactions in progress. The move — which strips away one of the key concessions granted to Tehran in exchange for reopening the Strait — marked a fresh challenge to the fragile US-Iran ceasefire. Oil markets reacted sharply: Brent crude rose about 3% to above $76/bbl, while WTI surged nearly 6% to above $72/bbl for the first time since June 30, reintroducing a geopolitical risk premium that had faded in recent sessions. Energy stocks were a rare pocket of strength in an otherwise lower tape.

### After-Hours Activity (Post-Close July 7)

Alphabet was reported to have joined a €411 million funding round for fusion-energy startup Proxima Fusion. Children's Place shares slipped about 2.8% in after-hours trading.
