## 2. MAJOR NEWS, EVENTS & ANNOUNCEMENTS

### A. Trump Reinstates Strait of Hormuz "Blockade," Imposes 20% Cargo Toll as US-Iran Ceasefire Collapses
Following a weekend of intensifying US-Iran hostilities — Iran fired on a commercial vessel transiting the Strait of Hormuz and its IRGC Navy declared the waterway closed, prompting a fourth round of US strikes (reportedly using one-way attack drones for the first time) on roughly 140 Iranian military sites, followed by Iranian retaliatory strikes on US and allied positions in Jordan, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman — President Trump posted on Truth Social Monday that the US would from that point be known as "the Guardian of the Hormuz Strait" and would be "reimbursed, at the rate of 20% on all cargo shipped," for providing security through the strait. CENTCOM said the blockade of Iranian shipping specifically would take effect Tuesday, July 14 at 4:00 p.m. ET, with other nations' cargo permitted to transit subject to the toll. Iran maintained the strait was fully closed; Trump disputed this, saying commercial traffic could continue. The International Maritime Organization pushed back within hours, stating "there is no legal basis through which to introduce mandatory tolls simply to transit through a strait." UBS commodities analyst Giovanni Staunovo said the market's focus would shift to "the number of inbound tankers, as a lower number could impact production," while Windward maritime intelligence analyst Michelle Wiese Bockmann noted "the only leverage Iran has is control of Hormuz" — a strait that carries roughly a fifth of global oil and gas trade. FHN Financial macro strategist Will Compernolle said many investors still viewed the exchange of strikes as posturing for leverage around the unraveled ceasefire framework rather than the start of a full war. The news pushed WTI up 9.4% to $78.14/bbl and Brent up 9.6% to $83.30/bbl, and was the single dominant driver of Monday's session across equities, rates, and FX.

### B. SK Hynix's Historic Plunge Triggers a Fresh Global Semiconductor Selloff
One trading day after its blockbuster $26.5 billion Nasdaq ADR debut, SK Hynix shares collapsed as much as 12-15% in Seoul — described by multiple outlets as the stock's steepest one-day fall in nearly two decades — triggering a KOSPI circuit breaker as the broader index tumbled roughly 8.9%. The reversal was driven by a brokerage note flagging downside risk to Q2 profit versus consensus, an unwind of arbitrage positioning against the richly-priced new US ADR (which itself fell 7.9% to $154.70), and the broader risk-off tone from the Iran escalation. Yuanta Securities global strategist Daniel Yoo captured the shift in sentiment: "Everybody's really confused about what's going to happen to the memory demand and where the fair price is." Samsung Electronics fell 8-9% in sympathy in Seoul. The rout spread to the Nasdaq, where Intel dropped roughly 4-4.6%, AMD fell 3-4%, Broadcom slid about 3%, Applied Materials lost around 4%, and Micron and SanDisk each fell roughly 4% as the prior week's AI-memory rally unwound; the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) fell about 3%. Nvidia was comparatively resilient, down only about 1.7%. The selloff also hit space-adjacent Nasdaq-100 names for a second straight session — SpaceX slid further toward its $135 IPO price, AST SpaceMobile fell about 5% and Rocket Lab dropped roughly 4%, compounded by a competing China rocket-launch milestone.

### C. Energy Stocks Rally on the Oil Spike, Cushioning the Dow
While tech sold off, energy was the day's standout sector, up roughly 1.3-1.4% at the sector level (and as much as 3.16% by some "fossil fuels" sub-index readings) as the Hormuz-driven oil spike lifted major producers: ExxonMobil rose 3.10%, ConocoPhillips gained 3.73%, and Chevron rose roughly 2-3%. Coverage explicitly credited energy-stock strength with cushioning the Dow's decline to just 0.26%, versus the Nasdaq's steeper 1.55% drop. The flip side showed up in airlines, where fuel-cost fears resurfaced (American, United, Delta and JetBlue all fell 3-5%) just as the sector heads into a summer earnings season already grappling with record fuel costs from Delta's July 10 report.

### D. Fed Governor Waller Warns a Rate Hike Is Still "Possible" if Inflation Stays Hot
In a speech Monday, Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said the Fed "shouldn't fight the last war" on inflation, warning that if the market gets "another hot reading on core inflation this week, then the FOMC will need to consider tightening monetary policy in the near term" — citing 2025 tariffs, Middle East energy prices, and AI-driven demand as inflation sources the Fed cannot ignore. The comments, which landed the same day as the oil spike, pushed the perceived odds of a July rate hike higher (one report cited a rise to roughly 39-50% from about 25% a week earlier) and were a key driver of Monday's rise in Treasury yields even as equities sold off — an unusual pattern in which bonds sold off (yields rose) alongside risk assets rather than rallying as a safe haven. Goldman Sachs raised its year-end 2026 headline PCE inflation forecast by 0.2 percentage point to 3.1% and lifted its recession probability to 30% (still not its base case) citing the oil shock; JPMorgan's Bob Michele said the Iran conflict is "not merely an inflation speed bump," while the bank's Jay Barry projected the 2-year and 10-year yields rising to 4.20% and 4.70%, respectively, by year-end.

### After-Hours / Post-Close Activity
Trading was comparatively quiet for mega-caps after the 4 p.m. close, with Nvidia and Meta little changed as the market held a defensive posture ahead of Tuesday's CPI report and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's congressional testimony. Spirits maker Brown-Forman disclosed its CEO is stepping down; shares were roughly flat in after-hours trading. No major large-cap earnings were scheduled after Monday's close — the next big catalyst is the wave of bank earnings due before Tuesday's open.
