## Executive Summary

US equity markets closed mixed on Wednesday, June 24, as investors digested a continuation of Monday's BoFA-driven rate shock while rotating into value, small-caps, and cyclicals. The Dow Jones gained +0.35% while the Russell 2000 outperformed (+1.23%), signaling a clear rotation out of rate-sensitive mega-cap tech into defensive and value names. The Nasdaq slipped -0.43% and the S&P 500 edged lower by -0.10%, with semiconductors remaining under pressure ahead of Micron Technology's closely-watched earnings after the bell.

After-hours, Micron (MU) delivered a decisive earnings beat — topping EPS estimates by ~23%, reporting DRAM revenue of $31.3B and NAND revenue of $9.9B, and guiding Q4 revenue $6–8B above Wall Street consensus. MU shares surged over +6% in extended trading.

Oil prices fell sharply (-4%), hitting their lowest level since before the US/Israel airstrikes against Iran in late February, partly in response to President Trump directing the DOJ to investigate oil companies over gasoline "gouging." Falling oil aided a modest drop in Treasury yields despite ongoing hawkish rate expectations. Gold fell -2.68% as the dollar held near 13-month highs.

## 2. Summary of Major Financial News & Events

### Oil Price Collapse — Trump DOJ Investigation
International benchmark **Brent crude fell -4.33% to $73.74/bbl**, hitting its lowest level since before the US and Israel launched airstrikes against Iran in late February 2026. **WTI (West Texas Intermediate) fell -3.92% to $70.34/bbl**.

**Catalyst:** President Donald Trump announced he has instructed the **Department of Justice to immediately investigate oil companies** over gasoline prices, accusing major producers of failing to pass through lower crude costs to consumers and alleging deliberate "gouging." The announcement sent energy prices into sharp decline as markets priced in potential regulatory/legal pressure on upstream producers.

The oil drop also reduced inflation expectations at the long end of the Treasury curve, contributing to a slight easing in the 10-Year yield.

### Fed Bank Stress Test Results
The Federal Reserve released the results of its **2026 annual bank stress test** at 4:00 PM EDT, evaluating 32 large US banks against a severe recession scenario:
- Unemployment rising +5.5 percentage points to a peak of **10%**
- **-30% decline in home prices**
- **-39% decline in commercial real estate values**
- Severe corporate bond spread widening and market volatility

**Result:** All 32 major US banks demonstrated sufficient capital to withstand the severe scenario while maintaining lending capacity.

**Important caveat:** The Fed had previously announced (February 2026) that this year's stress test results **will not trigger changes to capital requirements**, as the Fed is reviewing and potentially revising its testing framework through 2027. The results were broadly bullish for financial stocks, providing a tailwind for the Dow and large-cap financials.

### May New Home Sales — Disappointing Data
The Census Bureau and HUD released **May 2026 new home sales** data:
- **May 2026:** 580,000 annualized units (SAAR)
- **April 2026:** 626,000 (revised)
- **Month-over-Month:** **-7.3%** (missed forecasts)
- **Year-over-Year:** **-6.8%** vs May 2025's 622,000 units
- **Months of supply:** 10.3 months (up from 9.3 in April; elevated inventory)

The weakness was attributed to **rate shock and inflation squeeze on buyers**, with higher-for-longer rate expectations following the BofA/Deutsche Bank hike forecasts weighing on mortgage affordability. This contrasts with the simultaneously released NAR existing home sales report showing a +3.2% increase in May — a divergence that highlights selective demand weakness in new construction.

### Micron Technology (MU) — After-Hours Earnings Beat
Micron reported its fiscal Q3 2026 results after the bell — one of the most anticipated semiconductor earnings reports of the year. Results were significantly above expectations:

| Metric | Reported | Estimate | Beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| EPS (adjusted) | $25.11 | $20.39 | **+23%** |
| DRAM Revenue | $31.3B | $27.5B | **+14%** |
| NAND Revenue | $9.9B | $7.7B | **+29%** |
| Adjusted Gross Margin | 84.9% | 81.83% | **+307 bps** |
| Q4 Revenue Guidance | $49–51B | $43.2B | **+13–18%** |

Micron also **declared a quarterly cash dividend of $0.15/share**. MU stock surged **+6%+ in after-hours trading**.

**Context:** MU shares had surged +244% YTD in 2026, crossing a $1 trillion market cap, fueled by AI-driven demand for HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) chips. The massive beat on gross margins (84.9%) was the most critical data point for bulls, as it signals the premium pricing environment for AI memory is still intact and not deteriorating.

### Trump/Sen. Warren — Fed Policy Commentary
Senator Elizabeth Warren stated Wednesday that Federal Reserve Chair **Kevin Warsh** "has no good alternatives" on interest rate policy, noting that "Donald Trump has put Kevin Warsh and the Fed in a box." Warren's comments reflect the political pressure on the Fed from both sides: hawkish markets expecting hikes while the administration faces political sensitivity around higher rates.

2. **BofA/Deutsche Bank Hike Forecasts (Persistent Drag):** Despite oil easing, short-dated yields (2-Year at 4.24%) remain elevated as the market continues pricing near-term Fed tightening. There is no relief at the front end until hard data contradicts the hawkish thesis.
3. **Weak New Home Sales:** The -7.3% miss in May new home sales adds a data point supporting economic softening, which theoretically could moderate the Fed's hawkishness — however, the market did not fully price this in on June 24.
4. **Dollar Strength (Partial Offset):** The US Dollar Index (DXY) near 13-month highs provides an implicit tightening impulse, reducing the need for as aggressive Fed action at the margin — but this is a secondary consideration versus the inflation data.
