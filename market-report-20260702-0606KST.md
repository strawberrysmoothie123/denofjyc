## 2. MAJOR NEWS, EVENTS & ANNOUNCEMENTS

### A. June Jobs Report Badly Misses Forecasts, Sent a Day Early Ahead of Holiday

The Bureau of Labor Statistics' June Employment Situation report — released Thursday instead of the usual Friday because of the July 4 holiday — showed nonfarm payrolls rose just 57,000, roughly half the 115,000 Dow Jones consensus estimate and down sharply from a downwardly revised 129,000 in May (previously reported as 172,000). Combined April and May payrolls were revised down by a further 74,000. The unemployment rate nonetheless fell to 4.2% from 4.3%, but for the "wrong" reason: the labor-force participation rate dropped 0.3 percentage point to 61.5%, its lowest level since March 2021, meaning fewer workers were counted as looking for jobs rather than more finding them. Sector detail was mixed: professional and business services (+36,000), healthcare (+22,000) and social assistance (+25,000) posted gains, while leisure and hospitality shed 61,000 jobs on weaker seasonal hiring. Average hourly earnings rose 0.3% month-over-month (3.5% year-over-year). "This morning's data makes it difficult to envision a path toward a July Fed hike even if there is upside in the inflation data yet to be realized," said Ian Lyngen, head of US rates strategy at BMO Capital Markets, adding that "the probability of a July cut [has] also declined sharply, consistent with the read that payrolls have made it challenging to envision a summertime hike." The net effect was a market that priced out near-term hawkish risk without meaningfully pulling forward rate-cut bets — a "muddled" outcome that helps explain the sharp divergence between the Dow's record run and the Nasdaq's decline.

### B. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh Signals Inflation Risks Have Eased

Speaking as the jobs data was digested, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said inflation risks in the US economy had softened, a marginally dovish signal that pressured the dollar intraday and contributed to Treasury yields backing off an earlier test of 4.50% on the 10-year note. The comments reinforced the market's read that the Fed is unlikely to hike in July, while stopping short of committing to a near-term cut, leaving the July 28-29 FOMC meeting priced as a likely hold.

### C. Tesla Slides ~7% Despite Record Q2 Deliveries as Margin Fears and BYD Overtake Dominate

Tesla reported record second-quarter deliveries of 480,126 vehicles, up roughly 25% year-over-year and comfortably ahead of Wall Street's approximately 406,000-vehicle estimate, yet shares fell about 7% to roughly $395. Three factors drove the "beat-but-sell" reaction: (1) investors are focused on per-vehicle profit margins, which will not be disclosed until the July 22 earnings report, and are wary that the delivery surge came via price cuts and incentives; (2) the stock had already rallied roughly 12% into the print, leaving it vulnerable to a "sell-the-news" unwind; and (3) rival BYD reported 557,090 battery-electric deliveries for the quarter, reclaiming the global BEV sales lead from Tesla by a wide margin, while Cox Automotive projected a roughly 20% drop in Tesla's US sales following the expiration of the federal EV tax credit. Analysts framed the move as evidence that Tesla's competitive pressure in core vehicle markets is increasingly viewed as structural rather than cyclical.

### D. Semiconductors Extend Sell-Off for a Second Session

Chip stocks fell for a second straight day, continuing Wednesday's profit-taking after semiconductors' historic first-half surge (many names up more than 80% in H1 2026). The VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) dropped 4.5%, with Teradyne down 13.6% and KLA off 11.5% leading decliners; Nvidia slipped 1.4% and Micron fell 5.5%. The continued unwind kept Information Technology as the weakest S&P 500 sector (-1.84%) and was the primary drag on the Nasdaq Composite even as the broader market, led by the Dow, pushed to new highs.

### E. Sector Rotation Fuels Dow's Record Close

Communication Services (+2.62%) and Financials (+2.13%) led all S&P 500 sectors, with Consumer Discretionary (+0.82%) and Health Care (+0.52%) also higher, while Information Technology (-1.84%), Utilities (-1.30%) and Industrials (-1.06%) lagged. The rotation out of rate-sensitive megacap tech and into financials and communications names — which carry heavier weightings in the price-weighted Dow than in the Nasdaq — explains much of the gap between the Dow's 1.14% gain and the Nasdaq's 0.79% decline on the same trading day.

### F. Yen Whipsaws Near 40-Year Low on Suspected BOJ Intervention

The Japanese yen touched a fresh 40-year low of 162.83 per dollar before abruptly reversing to trade around 161.20, a roughly 1% intraday swing that traders attributed to suspected intervention by Japanese authorities. The move added to a volatile, headline-driven session across FX and rates markets.

### After-Hours Activity (Post-Close July 2)

No major S&P 500 earnings reports or market-moving after-hours announcements were scheduled following Thursday's close, consistent with the holiday-shortened week; desks instead focused on positioning ahead of the Friday, July 3 market closure and the long weekend.
