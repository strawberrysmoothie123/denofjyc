## 1. ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY

US equity markets posted a sharply bifurcated session on Thursday, June 25, 2026, as a sweeping rotation out of Magnificent 7 technology names into cyclicals and memory-chip leaders defined the tape. The S&P 500 finished essentially unchanged at 7,357.49 (-0.01%), masking violent intra-sector divergences: the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.46% under the weight of Apple's 6.1% plunge and Microsoft's 3.2% slide after both companies announced consumer hardware price hikes tied to surging memory costs, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average surged 0.40% powered by Caterpillar's near-6% rally and broad strength in industrials, healthcare, and financials. The dominant theme was Micron Technology's blowout fiscal Q3 earnings reported after Wednesday's close — revenue more than quadrupled year-over-year to $41.46 billion, sparking a memory-sector frenzy that lifted Micron (+12%) and SanDisk (+14.7%) even as downstream hardware makers like Apple and Microsoft absorbed the cost shock. Forward focus shifts decisively to Friday's May PCE inflation print, the Fed's preferred price gauge, where a hotter-than-expected reading could reinforce the new Warsh-era Fed's hawkish dot-plot signal of a potential rate hike before year-end.

## 2. MAJOR NEWS, EVENTS & ANNOUNCEMENTS

### A. Micron Technology Q3 Earnings Blowout (After-Hours, June 24)
Micron Technology reported fiscal third-quarter results after Wednesday's close that shattered Wall Street expectations across every metric. Adjusted earnings per share came in at **$25.11**, 21% above the consensus estimate of $20.78, while revenue hit **$41.46 billion** — more than quadrupling from $9.3 billion a year earlier — against analyst expectations near $36 billion. The AI-driven memory supercycle is delivering results well beyond earlier projections, with HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) and enterprise DRAM demand described as "insatiable" in management commentary. The stock surged **+17% in after-hours trading** on June 24 and extended those gains during Thursday's regular session, closing up approximately **+12%**. Semiconductor analysts at major firms raised price targets across the memory sector, with at least two calling the results a "generational earnings inflection." SanDisk, which also benefits from NAND flash demand, added **+14.7%** in sympathy. AVGO (Broadcom) and GOOGL (Alphabet) were cited by analysts as key read-throughs, given both companies are major memory consumers for AI infrastructure buildout.

### B. Apple Announces Consumer Hardware Price Hikes
Apple Inc. sent a shock through consumer tech markets by announcing price increases on MacBook and iPad product lines, explicitly citing higher memory component costs as the driver. The move — a rare, unscheduled mid-cycle repricing — signals that surging memory prices from the AI demand boom are flowing downstream to consumer hardware manufacturers. Apple shares closed down **-6.13%**, the stock's steepest single-session decline in months. Analysts were divided: bearish voices flagged demand elasticity risk and the potential for market-share erosion if competitors absorb rather than pass through costs; bulls argued the pricing power demonstrated validates Apple's premium brand and margin protection model. The announcement also raised questions about the iPhone 18 cycle's launch pricing later this year.

### C. Microsoft Announces Xbox Price Increases
Microsoft followed Apple's lead within hours, announcing price hikes on Xbox hardware and accessories, also attributing the increases to higher memory and component costs. Shares fell **-3.23%**, with the impact compounded by broader Magnificent 7 selling pressure. The dual announcements from Apple and Microsoft in a single session crystallized a new narrative for markets: the AI memory supercycle, while broadly bullish for semiconductor companies, is creating a meaningful cost headwind for consumer-facing hardware businesses. This represents an underappreciated second-order effect that analysts say will require reassessment of margins across the consumer electronics sector.

### D. US–Iran Peace Negotiations Progress
US–Iran peace talks advanced meaningfully on Thursday, with reports indicating a framework deal is closer than previously expected. The development triggered a fourth consecutive session of declines in crude oil prices, with WTI falling **below $70 per barrel**. The geopolitical risk premium that had been embedded in energy markets since the conflict intensified earlier this year continued to deflate. Easing energy prices reduced near-term inflation expectations and were cited by bond traders as a primary catalyst for the decline in Treasury yields. Cross-asset implications were substantial: lower oil supported equities broadly, pulled yields lower, weighed on the dollar, and pressured gold as an inflation hedge.

### E. Caterpillar and Industrials Lead Dow Higher
Caterpillar surged **+5.81%**, one of its largest single-session gains in the past year, contributing the bulk of the Dow Jones' 208-point advance. Reports suggested infrastructure spending momentum and improving global construction demand are driving a beat-and-raise expectations cycle for CAT. UnitedHealth Group added **+2.65%**, reflecting continued strength in managed care. Health care, industrials, and financials all outperformed, illustrating the rotational character of Thursday's session: money leaving Mag7 tech found a home in "old economy" names.

### F. After-Hours Activity (June 25 Close)
Following Thursday's close, equity index futures recovered from the session's soft close as investors reassessed the net positive implications of the memory earnings cycle for broader AI infrastructure spending. AVGO (Broadcom) and GOOGL (Alphabet) were specifically cited as in focus in post-close commentary, given each company's significant exposure to AI chip procurement. Specific after-hours price levels for individual stocks were not yet confirmed at report publication time.

### S&P 500 — 7,357.49 (-0.01%)

The S&P 500's near-flat close obscures the most dramatic sector divergence of the quarter. The index effectively flatlined because the selling in mega-cap technology was nearly perfectly offset by rallies across industrials, health care, energy equipment, and select semiconductors. The technology sector weighed approximately -0.5% on the index as Apple and Microsoft together account for over 12% of S&P 500 market cap; their combined declines of -6.1% and -3.2% are estimated to have subtracted roughly 55-60 index points. This was absorbed almost entirely by strength in industrials (Caterpillar alone contributed an estimated +15 index points), health care, and financials. The broad market's resilience in the face of two Magnificent 7 names falling sharply is interpreted by strategists as a sign of improving breadth — the rally no longer depends exclusively on a handful of mega-cap names. The VIX edging up 1.4% to 18.89 reflects some hedging activity around the upcoming PCE data rather than broad fear; the index remains well below levels that would signal systemic stress.

### Nasdaq Composite — 25,358.60 (-0.46%)

The Nasdaq bore the brunt of the tech selloff given its heavy weighting toward Apple, Microsoft, and other Magnificent 7 components. The Nasdaq 100 (which tracks the 100 largest non-financial Nasdaq stocks) fell a similar -0.5%. Despite Micron and SanDisk's dramatic gains providing partial offsets within the semiconductor sub-sector, the breadth of Mag7 weakness — seven of the group's members were under pressure as elevated memory costs ripple through to cloud and consumer hardware spending expectations — outweighed the positives. Strategists at growth-oriented shops noted that the 25,350-25,400 zone represents a key technical support cluster, and the Nasdaq's ability to hold this level on a tough day for tech is a mild constructive signal. The broader narrative shift — from pure AI-enthusiasm to a more discriminating view of which companies benefit vs. which bear costs from the AI buildout — is beginning to manifest in price action.

### Dow Jones Industrial Average — 51,848.90 (+0.40%)

The Dow's 208-point gain was the session's clearest expression of the rotational trade. As a price-weighted index with significant exposure to industrials, health care, and financials, the Dow is structurally positioned to benefit when investors rotate away from growth/tech. Caterpillar's +5.81% surge alone is estimated to have contributed approximately 220+ Dow points given its current share price level. UnitedHealth (+2.65%) and strength in financial components like Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan added further support. The Dow's outperformance is notable not just for a single session but as part of a broader trend: value and cyclical names have been quietly building momentum as the rate environment, while still elevated, shows signs of stabilizing. Market strategists note that the Dow's push toward and above 51,800 extends a technical breakout from earlier this month.

### Russell 2000 — 3,007.86 (+0.71%)

Small-cap stocks outperformed on the day, with the Russell 2000 gaining 0.71% — the best performance among major US indices. Small caps tend to be more domestically oriented and less exposed to the kind of global supply-chain and component-cost dynamics that are hurting mega-cap consumer hardware companies. The rotation toward small caps also reflects modest optimism around the PCE print: if Friday's data shows core inflation cooling toward 3.4% year-over-year, it reduces the near-term probability of a Fed rate hike and benefits small caps, which are more sensitive to domestic borrowing costs. The Russell 2000 holding above 3,000 is considered technically significant by chartists.

### VIX — 18.89 (+1.40%)

The CBOE Volatility Index edged higher by 1.4% to 18.89, reflecting modest demand for downside protection heading into Friday's pivotal PCE data release. The VIX at 18-19 remains in a historically "moderate" zone — well below the 25+ levels associated with genuine market stress and well above the complacency sub-15 readings seen at the index's peaks earlier this year. The VIX's mild uptick despite a broadly flat equity market suggests options traders are pricing in some tail risk around the PCE release: if core PCE prints materially above the 3.4% YoY consensus, it could validate the Fed's hawkish dot-plot revision and accelerate rate-hike pricing, potentially triggering a sharper equity selloff.

### Rate Market Narrative

Treasuries rallied across the curve on Thursday — meaning yields fell — driven by two converging forces: easing geopolitical risk from Middle East peace progress and the downstream disinflationary implications of crude oil falling below $70 per barrel.

The 10-year yield's move from 4.49% to 4.45% (a 4-basis-point decline) was the second consecutive session of falling yields after a brief spike in mid-June following the FOMC's hawkish dot-plot revision. The market appears to be pushing back against the Fed's implied hiking signal: bond traders are pricing in that lower oil prices — a major recent inflation driver — may forestall the need for additional tightening.

**Federal Reserve Context**: At its June 17 meeting, the FOMC under new Chair Kevin Warsh voted unanimously (12-0) to hold the federal funds rate at its current 3.50%–3.75% target range. However, the accompanying dot plot delivered a hawkish surprise: the median projection for the 2026 year-end rate shifted up to 3.8% from 3.4% in March projections, signaling that most FOMC members now see at least one additional hike as necessary. The committee also released a noticeably shorter policy statement, dispensing with explicit forward guidance — a stylistic shift under Warsh that reflects greater data-dependency and reduces the Fed's ability to be "talked into" a cut. The June 17 meeting also framed persistent inflation as partly driven by energy supply shocks related to Middle East tensions — which makes Thursday's oil decline potentially more consequential for future Fed thinking.

**Yield Curve Shape**: The curve remains positively sloped, with the 2s-10s spread near +29 basis points (4.45% minus ~4.16%). This marks a significant departure from the deeply inverted curve of 2023-2024, and strategists are watching whether the curve continues to steepen as the disinflation cycle plays out. A steeper curve would typically signal improving growth expectations and/or expectations for a sustained pause in Fed hikes.

**Institutional Rate Forecasts**: Wells Fargo economists, in commentary ahead of Friday's PCE release, warned that May PCE could print as high as +0.5% month-over-month due to energy-related cost passthrough. If realized at those levels, the inflationary pressure could push the 10-year back toward and beyond 4.49%, reversing Thursday's rally. Consensus on core PCE is +0.3% MoM and +3.4% YoY — still well above the Fed's 2% target. Markets are pricing approximately a 35-40% probability of one additional Fed rate hike before year-end, up from near-zero earlier in 2026.

### Dollar & FX Narrative

The US Dollar Index slipped 0.17% to 101.43, snapping a three-day winning streak and edging back from its strongest level since May 2025. The retreat was driven primarily by the twin effects of falling Treasury yields (reducing US rate-differential appeal) and easing geopolitical risk (reducing safe-haven demand for the dollar as Middle East tensions showed signs of diplomatic resolution).

**EUR/USD** advanced 0.19% to 1.1380, benefiting from the dollar's modest pullback. European risk assets broadly outperformed as lower oil prices are proportionally more beneficial for import-dependent eurozone economies. Dovish commentary from ECB officials earlier in the week, while still present in the background, was largely overshadowed by the geopolitical developments. Seasonal analysis from Forex.com had flagged potential for EUR/USD to rally back toward 1.18, and the pair's recovery from recent lows is being watched as a test of that thesis.

**USD/JPY** held near 161.82, effectively flat on the day, as the yen continued to languish near multi-decade lows against the dollar. Despite the DXY's mild weakness, the yen found no significant bid — a reflection of the Bank of Japan's persistent ultra-loose monetary policy stance and the massive interest rate differential between the US (3.50%–3.75%) and Japan (near-zero). USD/JPY near 161-162 remains in a zone that has historically drawn intervention warnings from Japanese officials, and market participants continue to monitor for any signs of coordinated FX intervention by the Ministry of Finance.

**Cross-Asset Implications**: The dollar's mild weakness provided a marginal tailwind for commodity-linked currencies (AUD, CAD, NOK) and emerging market FX, though the moves were modest. Gold, despite benefiting theoretically from a weaker dollar, slid **below $4,000 per troy ounce** toward its lowest levels in approximately eight months — a sign that the dominant forces on gold were expectations of a potential Fed rate hike and reduced geopolitical risk premium (both dollar-positive forces) that overwhelmed the currency effect. WTI crude oil's fourth consecutive decline **below $70/barrel** weighed on petrocurrencies such as the Canadian dollar and Norwegian krone. The broader takeaway for FX: the dollar is in a tactical consolidation phase, but the medium-term direction remains highly data-dependent on Friday's PCE release and the subsequent Fed reaction function.
