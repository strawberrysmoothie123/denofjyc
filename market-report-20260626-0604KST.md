## 2. MAJOR NEWS, EVENTS & ANNOUNCEMENTS

### 1. OpenAI IPO Delay Report — The Dominant Driver
The New York Times reported Friday that OpenAI's advisers presented CEO Sam Altman with two paths: delay the IPO until 2027 and target a $1 trillion valuation, or go public sooner at a meaningfully lower price. Altman reportedly told advisers that any reduction from the $1 trillion target was a non-starter — leaving the timeline ambiguous and rattling investor confidence in the broader AI trade. The report cited the cautionary tale of SpaceX (SPCX), which completed the largest IPO in history on June 12 at $135/share (raising $75 billion), surged to a peak of $225.64 intraday on June 16, then gave back nearly 27% of those gains to trade near $165 by late June. OpenAI's advisers reportedly cited that volatility as a reason for caution. The reaction was swift and broad: chip stocks, AI infrastructure names, and major OpenAI investor SoftBank Group were all hit hard, with SoftBank falling 9.2% on the day. Motley Fool's analysis flagged the event as a potential test of the AI bubble narrative: *"If the company that essentially launched the current AI era can't command the valuation it wants, what does that say about the premium baked into the entire sector?"*

### 2. Chip Stock Rout — AI Infrastructure Cost Concerns Compound
Friday's chip sell-off went beyond just the OpenAI headline. Analysts and institutional commentary pointed to rising AI infrastructure costs as a structural concern — suggesting the economics of large-scale AI model deployment may be more challenging than markets had previously priced in. **Intel** fell 3%, **SanDisk** plunged 10%, **Arm Holdings** lost nearly 4%, **Marvell Technology** dropped 5%, and **Micron Technology** fell more than 5% — a sharp reversal after Micron had surged Thursday on blowout Q3 earnings (strong AI memory demand beat expectations significantly). **Advanced Micro Devices** slid 2.6%. **Nvidia** and **Alphabet** were the two Magnificent Seven names that failed to participate in a broader partial recovery among mega-caps.

### 3. Apple Rebounds; Mag7 Diverges
Apple (AAPL) climbed approximately 3% Friday, recovering most of Thursday's sharp slide, which had been triggered by the company's announcement of price increases across its iPhone, MacBook, and iPad lines, citing higher component costs — particularly chips. The rebound suggested investors viewed the selloff as an overreaction to manageable pricing power dynamics. Five of the seven Magnificent Seven stocks traded in the green on the day, with only Nvidia and Alphabet declining alongside the chip complex.

### 4. Microsoft's Xbox Price Increases
As part of the broader consumer electronics pricing theme, Microsoft also announced price increases on the Xbox console lineup on Thursday (June 25), echoing Apple's rationale of elevated component and manufacturing costs. This reinforced investor concern about margin pressure across the consumer tech ecosystem from persistently high chip input costs.

### 5. Russell Reconstitution (Effective After Close)
FTSE Russell's semi-annual index reconstitution — the annual reshuffling of which stocks belong in the Russell 1000 and Russell 2000 — took effect after Friday's close. According to LSEG, the 2026 reconstitution reflected improving market breadth beyond mega caps, with technology and industrials companies leading movement into the Russell 1000 while a diverse mix of emerging companies entered the Russell 2000. Morgan Stanley's institutional management arm noted the reconstitution "blurs style lines and raises concentration risk" but broadly viewed the changes as reflective of genuine economic momentum in small and mid-cap segments. The reconstitution contributed to elevated volumes and some defensive rotation into small caps during the Friday session.

### 6. SpaceX (SPCX) Context — The IPO Cautionary Tale
SpaceX's June 12 IPO ($135/share, raising $75 billion — the largest IPO in history) served as the backdrop for OpenAI's IPO indecision. After peaking intraday at $225.64 on June 16, SPCX retreated to approximately $165 by late June, down roughly 27% from its peak (though still ~22% above its IPO price). OpenAI's advisers cited this volatility, along with broader AI share price turbulence, as factors counseling a delayed, higher-valuation listing rather than a rushed debut into a volatile market.

### After-Hours Activity
No major US earnings reports were released after hours on Friday, June 26. The primary post-close event was the effective rebalancing of the FTSE Russell US indexes, which generated significant ETF and index-fund trading activity in extended hours. The prior session's (June 25) Micron blowout earnings, though initially celebrated after hours Thursday, ultimately contributed to downward pressure on the broader chip sector by Friday's close as investors grew concerned that Micron's strong AI-driven demand was occurring at the expense of higher infrastructure costs for end-users.

Investors have increasingly viewed the Dow as a defensive port during the current tech rotation, with strategists at several firms noting the index's composition makes it a relative beneficiary of any sustained move away from AI-speculative holdings toward quality cyclicals.

### Russell 2000 — 3,007.86 (+0.71%)
The Russell 2000 was Friday's clear outperformer, rising 0.71% while large-cap tech indexes fell. LSEG noted that the 2026 reconstitution's additions skew toward industrials and emerging technology companies — sectors where small-cap earnings have shown improving fundamental momentum relative to their large-cap peers. Morgan Stanley's investment management division flagged the reconstitution as adding near-term concentration risk but broadly affirmed the strong-small-cap performance thesis for H2 2026.

A VIX below 20 generally signals moderate market stress, but the week's 4.6% Nasdaq decline and the abrupt AI narrative shift drove options market participants to bid up near-term protection. The risk of further IPO-related volatility — with Anthropic also having filed SEC paperwork for what is expected to be another mega-IPO — is likely keeping a floor under the VIX even as broader market breadth remains constructive.

### Bond Market Narrative

Treasury yields were broadly flat on Friday, with the bond market largely pricing in a consolidation period following the June 17 FOMC outcome. The curve remains gently upward-sloping — a notable shift from the deeply inverted curve that characterized 2024–2025 — reflecting both the Fed's achieved normalization of the short end and markets' growing expectation of further tightening in H2 2026.

At the June 17 meeting, Kevin Warsh's Federal Open Market Committee held the federal funds rate at 3.50%–3.75% but removed key language signaling a bias toward cuts. The revised dot plot now shows a median end-2026 fed funds rate of 3.80% (up from 3.4% in March projections), signaling one additional 25 basis-point hike before year-end. Nine of 18 FOMC members project at least one hike; six project two hikes. The Fed also revised its 2026 inflation forecast meaningfully higher — headline PCE to 3.6% (from 2.7% in March) and core PCE to 3.3% (from 2.7%) — reflecting persistent stickiness in services inflation, shelter costs, and the passthrough of elevated import and component prices.

Warsh notably did not submit his own dot, stating he encourages colleagues to do so but declined to specify a rate path himself. He outlined five task forces tasked with overhauling the Fed's monetary policy operations, communications strategy, data sources, labor market modeling, and inflation methodology — signaling a potentially significant long-run shift in how the institution approaches policy. The market initially interpreted this ambiguity as marginally hawkish, contributing to the post-meeting yield spike seen in the June 18–19 sessions.

The current 10-year yield of 4.40% sits meaningfully above the top of the Fed Funds target range (3.75%), implying the bond market has already priced in cumulative tightening and is watching for any upside surprise in PCE or labor data that could accelerate the July–September hiking timeline. The 2-year Treasury at 4.13% — below the 10yr — confirms the curve's restored normalcy but also suggests the market believes the terminal rate may not go significantly higher than current levels.

### Dollar & FX Narrative

The dollar has strengthened 2.17% over the past month and is up approximately 4.06% over the past 12 months, driven primarily by the hawkish pivot at the June 17 FOMC meeting and markets pricing in 62% probability of a September rate hike.

**EUR/USD** rose 0.13% to 1.1385 on Friday, recovering modestly from levels near June 2025 lows. The euro has been under consistent pressure from the combination of US dollar strength and a relatively more dovish ECB posture in the face of slowing European growth. The EUR is the largest DXY component (57.6% weighting), meaning euro weakness has been the primary driver of DXY strength year-to-date. The pair remains technically fragile; a sustained break below 1.13 would open a run toward 1.10.

**USD/JPY** edged lower on Friday to 161.60 (yen slightly stronger), as verbal intervention risk from Japanese authorities remains elevated. The yen has been trading near its weakest levels since 1986, with the Bank of Japan maintaining ultra-loose policy while the Federal Reserve signals higher-for-longer rates. Repeated verbal warnings from Japanese Finance Ministry officials have had only temporary effect, and market participants largely view the 160–162 range as a friction zone, not a durable floor. Any surprise BOJ rate action or US recession signal could trigger a sharp yen rally from these historically stretched levels.

**Cross-asset implications:** Dollar strength has weighed on gold and commodities priced in USD, though modest Friday dollar weakness provided some relief. Emerging market currencies have faced persistent pressure from the combination of a strong dollar and hawkish Fed narrative — a dynamic that could intensify if the September hike materializes. Oil markets have been tracking Middle East geopolitical developments alongside dollar dynamics, with any dollar weakening typically supportive of crude prices at the margin.
