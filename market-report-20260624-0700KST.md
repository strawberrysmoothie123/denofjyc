## 2. MAJOR NEWS, EVENTS & ANNOUNCEMENTS

### A. Strait of Hormuz Opens — Oil Collapses 4%+

The most consequential macro event of the session came from Washington when President Trump announced that Iran had confirmed there would be **no tolls, insurance surcharges, or transit charges** for commercial vessels passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The statement dramatically unwound the geopolitical risk premium that had been embedded in crude oil prices for weeks. Brent crude futures fell 4.33% to settle at **$73.74/bbl**, and WTI crude dropped 3.92% to **$70.34/bbl**, briefly touching an intraday low of $69.63. Energy sector stocks declined broadly in sympathy, though the pass-through to the broader market was ultimately constructive: falling oil directly reduces near-term inflation expectations, providing relief to the bond market and softening the case for an imminent Fed rate hike. The energy-driven move was the primary catalyst that drove Treasury yields lower across the curve and underpinned the outperformance of the Dow's more defensive composition versus the Nasdaq's growth-heavy tilt.

*Why it matters:* The Strait of Hormuz carries roughly 20% of globally traded oil. A durable reopening removes one of the key upside risks to the Fed's revised PCE inflation forecast of 3.6% for 2026 (up sharply from the 2.7% March projection). If energy deflation proves sustained, the probability of a September rate hike — currently priced at ~68% — could recede materially.

### B. Apple Price Increases Drag Nasdaq — China Adds to Concern

Apple (AAPL) fell **–0.74% to $291.00** after officially announcing price increases across its iPad and MacBook lineups, citing "skyrocketing memory and storage costs" driven by the semiconductor industry's pivot toward high-bandwidth memory for AI data centers over consumer electronics. The company's acknowledgment that AI infrastructure demand is directly cannibalizing consumer chip supply was a notable admission, and it resonated painfully across the consumer tech complex. Compounding the pressure, May iPhone shipments in China fell **19% year-over-year**, reducing Apple's regional market share to approximately 11% — its lowest level in years. APAC Research and KGI Securities both downgraded the stock to "Hold" following the announcements. Apple's weighting in the Nasdaq (approximately 8%) was sufficient to anchor the index even as semiconductors partially recovered from Tuesday's sell-off. Technology Equipment as a sector fell **–1.05%**.

*Analyst context:* Despite the near-term headwinds, Goldman Sachs maintained its "Buy" rating on Apple with a $340 price target, arguing that the price increases, while optically negative for unit demand, will materially improve gross margin trajectories in consumer hardware. The divergence between institutional views reflects broader uncertainty about whether Apple can execute its AI monetization roadmap with iPhones priced out of reach for its most important growth market.

### C. After-Hours: Micron Technology — Blowout Quarter, Shares Surge ~14%

After the market close, Micron Technology (MU) reported **fiscal Q3 2026 results that shattered estimates across every metric:**

| Metric | Reported | Consensus Estimate |
|---|---|---|
| Earnings Per Share (non-GAAP) | $25.11 | $20.83 |
| Revenue | $41.5B | $35.75B |
| Gross Margin (non-GAAP) | 84.9% (record) | ~75% est. |
| GAAP Net Income | $28.24B ($24.67/diluted share) | — |

Micron issued Q4 revenue guidance of **$49B–$51B**, against analyst expectations of $43.2B. GAAP net income for the quarter reached $28.24B — a staggering increase from $1.885B in the year-ago period. The company also declared a quarterly dividend of $0.15/share and announced $100B in new AI-related memory contracts. HBM4 production ramp was cited as ahead of schedule.

Shares surged **approximately 14% in after-hours trading to ~$1,190**, extending a year-to-date gain of approximately 325%. The results confirm that the AI-driven memory supercycle continues to accelerate beyond even the most optimistic models. The beat was broad-based and included record DRAM and NAND pricing power driven by hyperscaler demand for high-bandwidth memory in AI training infrastructure.

*Why it matters:* Micron's results have direct implications for the entire semiconductor supply chain. Peers including SK Hynix, Samsung, NVIDIA, and Marvell Technology are all positioned to benefit from ongoing AI memory demand. The magnitude of the beat also validates the thesis that AI capex has not plateaued — and materially challenges the bear case that has been building around AI infrastructure valuations after Tuesday's sector-wide sell-off.

### D. Context: Tuesday's Tech Sell-Off and Recovery Attempt

Wednesday's session unfolded against the backdrop of a significant tech sell-off on Tuesday, June 23, which was triggered by a Bank of America research note warning of elevated rate-hike risk combined with a sharp decline in Asian semiconductor markets overnight. The Nasdaq fell heavily on Tuesday, and Wednesday saw an initial recovery attempt that ultimately failed for tech names as Apple's announcements were digested. The Dow and Russell, however, completed a genuine sector rotation that reflected investor preference for value and rate-sensitive small caps over growth in the near term.
