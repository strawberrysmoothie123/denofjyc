## 1. ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY

US equities kicked off the third quarter on a mixed-to-lower note Wednesday, July 1, as investors booked profits following the best first-half rally since 2020: the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped a marginal 0.03% to 52,305.24 after touching a fresh intraday record above 52,700, the S&P 500 fell 0.22% to 7,483.23, and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.66% to 26,040.03 as chipmakers led the pullback, with Micron and SanDisk tumbling on profit-taking after their explosive first-half surges. The dominant theme was rotation and de-risking after an extraordinary Q2 — a soft ADP employment print (+98,000 vs. ~112,000 consensus), a fresh short position against AI-rally darling Caterpillar disclosed by "Big Short" investor Michael Burry (shares fell as much as 6.7%), and noncommittal remarks from new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh at the ECB's Sintra forum all weighed on sentiment, though Meta Platforms bucked the trend, surging roughly 8-11% on reports it plans to launch a cloud-computing business selling excess AI compute capacity. Treasury yields firmed modestly (10-year back to 4.40%) and the dollar index extended its advance to around 101.4 as the yen slid to a fresh four-decade low past 162/dollar, while attention now turns to Thursday's early-released June jobs report (due to the July 4 holiday) and continued Fed commentary from Sintra.

## 2. MAJOR NEWS, EVENTS & ANNOUNCEMENTS

### A. Chip Stocks Lead Profit-Taking as Q3 Opens

After the Nasdaq's 22%+ Q2 surge and the semiconductor sector's best quarter on record, traders rotated out of the group's biggest winners on the first trading day of the second half. Micron Technology dropped roughly 7-8% and SanDisk slumped 9-10% — both still up more than 260% and 850%, respectively, year-to-date — while Western Digital also fell sharply. The pullback was broad-based within tech: seven of the S&P 500's 11 sectors finished in the red even as Technology as a group ultimately closed higher (+2.38%, with the Information Technology Select Sector SPDR up 2.8%), reflecting sharp intra-sector dispersion between memory-chip names taking profits and other tech constituents (including Meta) rallying. Strategists framed the move as a healthy, overdue pause after a first half in which several AI-linked chip names had risen 150-850%, rather than a change in the underlying AI-capex narrative.

### B. Michael Burry Discloses First-Ever Short Against Caterpillar, Rattling AI-Adjacent Industrials

"Big Short" investor Michael Burry revealed via his Substack newsletter that he had initiated a short position in Caterpillar at $1,060.98 — his first-ever bearish bet against a stock he has historically favored on the long side — alongside expanded short positions in Nvidia ($198.09), Applied Materials ($729.40), Tesla ($416.22), and the SOXX semiconductor index ($642.80). Burry argued the market is paying roughly 53x earnings for Caterpillar's normalized earnings power, an unsustainable multiple built on its recasting as an AI-infrastructure proxy after an 86% first-half gain. Caterpillar shares fell between 4.4% and 6.9% intraday on the disclosure, dragging on the Dow and offsetting gains elsewhere in the index — directly explaining why the blue-chip average, despite an earlier record intraday high near 52,700, closed essentially flat. The episode reinforces a broader debate among strategists (including HSBC's second-half "pain trade" note flagged in yesterday's session) about stretched valuations in AI-adjacent industrials and semiconductors after their historic first-half run.

### C. Soft ADP Jobs Report Sets Cautious Tone Ahead of Thursday's Payrolls

The ADP National Employment Report showed private payrolls rose just 98,000 in June, below the roughly 112,000 consensus and down from an unrevised 122,000 in May, with nearly half the gains concentrated in education/health services while leisure and hospitality logged a sixth straight month of weak hiring. The soft print added to a cautious tone into Thursday's early-released (pre-holiday) official June nonfarm payrolls report, where consensus sits around 115,000 versus May's 172,000. Markets treated the data as a two-sided signal: soft enough to support hopes for eventual Fed easing, but not weak enough to change the near-term policy calculus given the Fed's stated focus on inflation.

### D. Fed Chair Warsh Offers No Forward Guidance at ECB's Sintra Forum

Speaking alongside ECB President Christine Lagarde, Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey, and Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem at the European Central Bank's central banking forum in Sintra, Portugal, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh declined to signal the July policy path, reiterating only that "prices are too high" and that the FOMC was readying for a "good family fight" at its July 28-29 meeting. Notably, Warsh acknowledged that inflation expectations and near-term inflation risks had eased somewhat in recent weeks — a marginally softer tone than his earlier hawkish messaging — which stocks initially read as mildly dovish, helping the S&P 500 and Dow bounce off session lows in the immediate aftermath of his remarks even as the Nasdaq stayed under pressure from the chip sell-off.

### After-Hours Activity (Post-Close July 1)

No major S&P 500 earnings releases or market-moving after-hours announcements were reported following Wednesday's close; trading desks shifted focus to positioning ahead of Thursday's early jobs report and the market's early close/holiday closure around the July 4 weekend.

### S&P 500 (7,483.23, -0.22%)

The S&P 500 pulled back modestly after notching a string of record highs to close out Q2, as investors took profits in the semiconductor names that had powered the quarter's rally. Communication services and financials were bright spots — Meta Platforms' roughly 8-11% surge on cloud-business reports was a standout — while seven of 11 sectors finished lower, led down by consumer-facing and materials-adjacent names caught up in the Caterpillar-driven industrials wobble. Strategists characterized the session as consolidation rather than a trend reversal, noting that Q1 earnings growth of roughly 18% year-over-year continues to underpin valuations even as forward multiples in AI-linked pockets of the market remain a focus of caution following the historic first-half advance.

### Nasdaq Composite (26,040.03, -0.66%)

The Nasdaq was the session's weakest major index as memory-chip stocks gave back some of their extraordinary first-half gains: Micron fell roughly 7-8% and SanDisk slumped 9-10%, both still sitting on triple-digit percentage year-to-date gains. The pullback followed a record quarter for the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and reflected profit-taking rather than a shift in the AI-infrastructure demand narrative, with several desks noting the memory/storage cohort in particular had become the most crowded, highest-beta corner of the AI trade after gains exceeding 260-850% in six months. Meta's cloud-business news provided a partial offset within mega-cap tech, but was not enough to keep the index positive.

### Dow Jones Industrial Average (52,305.24, -0.03%)

The Dow scaled a fresh intraday record above 52,700 before fading to close essentially flat, a pattern directly attributable to Caterpillar's 4.4-6.9% intraday slide after Michael Burry disclosed a short position in the stock. Caterpillar had been one of the best-performing Dow components in the first half (+86%) on its recasting as an AI-infrastructure proxy, making it an outsized swing factor for the price-weighted index. Ex-Caterpillar, the Dow's other 29 components were broadly mixed-to-higher, meaning the index's flat finish masks what was, for most of its constituents, a modestly positive session.

### Russell 2000 (3,012.59, -0.39%)

Small caps slipped alongside the broader market's chip-driven profit-taking, giving back a portion of a historic first half in which the index posted its best H1 performance since 1991. The Russell's decline was milder in percentage terms than the Nasdaq's, consistent with small caps' lower direct exposure to the memory-chip cohort at the center of Wednesday's sell-off, though soft ADP jobs data and rate-sensitivity concerns (small caps carry more floating-rate debt) also weighed modestly on sentiment.

### VIX (16.41, -3.53%)

The Cboe Volatility Index extended its post-Q2 decline, easing to 16.41 from Tuesday's 17.01 close, even as the S&P 500 and Nasdaq both fell on the session — an unusual but not unheard-of divergence that traders attributed to the modest, orderly nature of the pullback (no panic selling) combined with options positioning that had built up ahead of the quarter-end volatility unwind. The continued grind lower in volatility suggests markets read Wednesday's profit-taking as rotation within a still-intact bull trend rather than the start of a broader de-risking event.

The dollar index extended its advance to around 101.4, on track for a second straight monthly gain, as firmer Treasury yields and a Fed that remains reluctant to signal near-term easing continued to support the greenback. The most acute FX story remained the yen, which weakened beyond 162 per dollar — its softest level since 1986 — as Japan's fiscally dovish stance widened the effective yield gap with the US; traders increasingly floated the holiday-thinned Friday session (ahead of the US July 4 closure) as a window where Tokyo's Ministry of Finance could step in with intervention, given amplified impact in lower-liquidity conditions. The euro slipped modestly to 1.1407 as softer-than-expected Eurozone inflation prints limited the single currency's ability to keep pace with the dollar's broader firmness. Cross-asset, the yen's slide and intervention speculation produced a brief, offsetting dynamic in commodities: gold rebounded roughly 2% off near eight-month lows to around $4,090/oz intraday as dollar-weakness bets tied to potential BOJ/MOF action provided a haven bid, even though the DXY itself closed higher on the session — underscoring how intervention speculation can move gold independently of the broader dollar trend. Oil remained subdued near its multi-quarter lows as the de-escalation in Strait of Hormuz tensions continued to cap crude's upside.
