## 2. MAJOR NEWS, EVENTS & ANNOUNCEMENTS

### A. US-Iran Conflict Escalates Further, But Markets Shrug It Off
The US carried out fresh airstrikes on approximately 90 military and strategic targets across Iran early Thursday, the largest such operation since the ceasefire Trump declared "over" on Wednesday, and Tehran responded with missile and drone strikes targeting US interests across the Gulf. Despite the intensification of the conflict, equities rose rather than sold off, a marked departure from Wednesday's risk-off reaction — strategists attributed the shift to investors having already priced in a substantial geopolitical risk premium a day earlier, alongside the emergence of an offsetting, equity-friendly catalyst (the semiconductor rally) that dominated positioning. Iran was also reported to have rushed out roughly 11 million barrels of crude in the prior 24 hours amid concern over a potential US-imposed blockade of its ports, underscoring that the underlying conflict remains unresolved even as Thursday's tape looked calmer.

### B. Oil Reverses Lower Despite Conflict, on Surprise Inventory Build
Crude prices fell even as the conflict deepened: WTI settled near $72.01/bbl (-2.1%) and Brent slid a similar magnitude to roughly $76.39/bbl, both retracing a portion of Wednesday's sharp geopolitically-driven surge (WTI +4.4%, Brent +5.3%). The reversal was driven primarily by an EIA report showing a surprise ~3-million-barrel build in US commercial crude inventories for the week ended July 4 — the first weekly stockpile increase since April, against consensus expectations for a drawdown — which deflated the risk premium the market had built into prices a day earlier. The energy sector nonetheless still outperformed on a relative basis (XLE +2.8%) as crude remains well above pre-escalation levels and headline risk from the Gulf persists.

### C. Semiconductors Lead a Broad Rally on Micron Investment News and SK Hynix Anticipation
Chip and memory stocks staged their sharpest rebound in weeks, with the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) up more than 5% and the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) climbing 2.5%. Micron Technology jumped more than 5% after announcing a major new US investment commitment aimed at strengthening the domestic semiconductor supply chain, while Sandisk (+7%+), AMD (+7.1%) and Marvell Technology (+6.5%) also rallied sharply. Sentiment in the group was further buoyed by anticipation of SK Hynix's Nasdaq ADR debut: the South Korean memory giant finalized pricing on its roughly $28 billion offering Thursday (scaled back modestly from an initial ~$29.4 billion target amid recent share-price volatility), with when-issued trading under ticker "SKHYV" set to begin Friday, July 10, ahead of regular trading under "SKHY" on July 13 — a listing analysts have flagged as one of the largest-ever US share offerings by a foreign issuer and a bellwether for AI-driven memory demand.

### D. Jobless Claims Beat Expectations, Labor Market Still Cooling Gradually
Initial jobless claims for the week ended July 4 came in at 215,000, below both the prior week's revised 217,000 and consensus estimates of roughly 218,000, while the four-week moving average eased to 218,750. Continuing claims rose modestly to 1.814 million but remained slightly below forecasts. The data reinforced a narrative of a labor market that is cooling gradually rather than deteriorating sharply — soft enough to keep the Fed's "wait and see" posture intact ahead of the July 28-29 FOMC meeting, but not weak enough to reignite recession concerns, a combination strategists characterized as a mild net positive for risk assets.

### After-Hours Activity (Post-Close July 9)
Post-close trading was relatively quiet compared to Wednesday's earnings-driven moves, with attention shifting to Friday's calendar: Delta Air Lines (DAL) is set to report before Friday's open, with consensus calling for EPS near $1.47 (down roughly 30% year-over-year) on revenue of about $18.9 billion, an early read on summer travel demand and airline cost pressures. Market participants continued to position ahead of SK Hynix's Friday when-issued debut, with reports of the $28 billion ADR offering being multiple times oversubscribed pointing to strong initial demand.
